This Season in Scripture — The Day Before the Question

On the wet road from Bethel.

Scripture dates God's word to Zechariah to tomorrow, the fourth day of this month (Zechariah 7:1) — so around this season, men from Bethel are on the road to Jerusalem, cloaks damp, sandals heavy with hill-country mud, carrying one honest question about fasting. God's answer reaches past the fast: “Render true judgments and show kindness and mercy to one another” (Zechariah 7:9, ESV).

Through their eyes, on preparation day.

Today is Friday, and by mid-afternoon every village hurries. A farmer leaves the sowing early — Kislev dark comes fast. Bread is baked before sundown, lentils cooked ahead, water jars filled, lamps trimmed and filled with new olive oil, animals fed. Children help carry and clean, learning Sabbath by doing it beside their parents, and the Shema waits for evening (Deuteronomy 6:7). Then the work stops.

The land under rain.

Around this season the rains keep turning dry wadis into rushing streams, the Jordan begins to rise, and snow caps Hermon, seen far north on a clear morning between showers.

What is coming.

Hanukkah begins at sundown Friday, December 4 — 21 days away, on 25 Kislev. For now the nearer preparation is rest tonight, as God gave it.

Friend, finish today's work, lay the tools down, and receive the rest God built into the week.
