Introduction — Before You Start

Friend, let me tell you plainly what you're holding and why it's here.

When God saved me, He didn't just forgive me. He adopted me. And an adopted man has a family history he never grew up hearing. For over thirty years I ran from God, and when He finally got hold of me, I wanted to know everything — where this faith came from, who carried it before me, and how it survived people far stronger than the ones who tried to kill it. These twelve histories are the fruit of that hunger, written for folks like me: ordinary believers who love the Lord and want to know the story of their own family.

Inside you'll find the story of the church Christ built and the people who gathered in it. You'll read how God gave us His Word and kept it safe through fire, prison, and centuries of copying by hand. You'll stand at the cross and see what actually happened there. You'll trace baptism and the Lord's Supper from the Bible's pages to the table in front of you today. You'll meet men and women who prayed through the night and sang in prison cells. You'll walk through the Reformation, when God recovered the gospel for common people. You'll look honestly at the religions of the world and see why the gospel stands alone. And you'll see marriage the way God designed it — from a garden in Eden to a wedding feast that hasn't happened yet.

One thread runs through every page: men fail, and God doesn't. That's the bad news and the good news of church history, same as it's the bad news and the good news of my own life. Kings tried to stamp this faith out. The church herself drifted and had to be called back. And through all of it, the Lord carried His people — not because they were strong, but because He is faithful.

Here's how to use this book. Read one history at a time. Keep your Bible open beside it — everything here can be checked, and it ought to be. There's no schedule and no test at the end. If you miss a week, don't pile up guilt trying to catch up; just pick it up where you left it. Read it with your husband or your wife if you can. Talk about it. Let it do its slow work.

My prayer is simple: that when you close this book, you won't just know more history — you'll trust Him more. The same God who carried them will carry you. He carried me, and I was not easy to carry.

— Lewis Fuller

  • The Persecuted Church — A HistoryThe suffering church from Stephen to the present day, and how God has carried His witnesses.

  • The Religions of the World — And How Christianity DiffersAn honest look at what others believe, and why the gospel stands alone.
