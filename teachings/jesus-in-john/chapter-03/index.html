John 3 — You Must Be Born Again: Nicodemus by Night and the Son Lifted Up

Jesus in John — A Chapter-by-Chapter Commentary

  • Teachings
  • Gospel of John
  • Commentary

Jesus in this chapter

In John 3, Jesus reveals Himself as the Teacher who will not accept a religious résumé — who tells Israel's teacher that he must be born again — and as the Son lifted up like the serpent in the wilderness, so that whoever believes in Him should not perish but have eternal life. This chapter holds the most loved sentence ever spoken, and it is spoken to a man who came in the dark.

Commentary

Nicodemus comes by night (3:1–8). "Now there was a man of the Pharisees named Nicodemus, a ruler of the Jews. This man came to Jesus by night." Nicodemus is everything religion can make a man — a Pharisee, a ruler, a member of the council, "the teacher of Israel" — and he comes by night, half in courage and half in hiding. Give him this much credit: he came. Many of his colleagues only plotted. Nicodemus comes with a compliment that is also a confession: "Rabbi, we know that you are a teacher come from God, for no one can do these signs that you do unless God is with him."

Jesus honors none of his credentials. He goes straight past the compliment to the need: "Truly, truly, I say to you, unless one is born again he cannot see the kingdom of God." That is the bad news for every religious résumé ever written: flesh gives birth to flesh, and no amount of polishing the old life turns it into the new one. Nicodemus takes it with literal ears: "How can a man be born when he is old? Can he enter a second time into his mother's womb and be born?" Jesus answers with the word that unlocks the puzzle. The Greek is anōthen (ἄνωθεν) — again, and also from above. Nicodemus hears "born again" and thinks of a second physical birth; Jesus means "born from above." The word carries both, and both are true: the new birth is a genuine second beginning, and it comes down from heaven before it ever shows up on earth. "Unless one is born of water and the Spirit, he cannot enter the kingdom of God. That which is born of the flesh is flesh, and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit." Water and the Spirit — Ezekiel's old promise, a clean heart and a new spirit given by God (Ezekiel 36:25–27). "Do not marvel that I said to you, 'You must be born again.' The wind blows where it wishes, and you hear its sound, but you do not know where it comes from or where it goes. So it is with everyone who is born of the Spirit." You cannot control the wind, schedule it, or take credit for it. You can only hear it move and see what it moves. So it is with the Spirit's work in a human life.

The Son lifted up (3:9–15). Nicodemus asks, "How can these things be?" and Jesus answers with gentle astonishment — "Are you the teacher of Israel and yet you do not understand these things?" — and then rises from earthly things to heavenly ones. "No one has ascended into heaven except he who descended from heaven, the Son of Man." Only the One who came down can tell us what is up there. "And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up, that whoever believes in him may have eternal life." In Numbers 21, Israel, bitten by fiery serpents for their rebellion, was healed by one remedy: look at the bronze serpent Moses lifted on a pole, and live. The bitten did not have to crawl to it, touch it, pay for it, or understand it. They had to look. Jesus says His cross will work the same way. He will be lifted up — John's word for crucifixion and glorification in one — and the perishing who look to Him in faith will live.

God so loved the world (3:16–21). Then the sentence:

"For God so loved the world, that he gave his only Son, that whoever believes in him should not perish but have eternal life." — John 3:16 (ESV)

Take it slowly. God so loved — the love is measured not by a feeling but by a gift. The world — not the deserving, not the clean, not the tribe — this rebellious, darkness-loving world. That he gave his only Son — the gift is a Person, and the giving runs all the way to a cross. That whoever believes in him — the door is as wide as "whoever," and the key is believing, receiving, trusting. Should not perish but have eternal life — the two destinies, and faith is the dividing line. "For God did not send his Son into the world to condemn the world, but in order that the world might be saved through him." The Son's first errand is rescue, not sentencing. But John will not soften the other side: "Whoever believes in him is not condemned, but whoever does not believe is condemned already, because he has not believed in the name of the only Son of God. And this is the judgment: the light has come into the world, and people loved the darkness rather than the light because their works were evil." The judgment is not mainly that men did wrong in the dark. It is that when the Light came, they loved the dark better and would not come to the light, "lest his works should be exposed." That is why men refuse Christ — not lack of evidence, but love of the dark. "But whoever does what is true comes to the light, so that it may be clearly seen that his works have been carried out in God." The born-again life does not fear the light. It walks into it.

The friend of the Bridegroom (3:22–36). The chapter closes with John the Baptist's last great testimony, and it is a model for every minister who ever lived. His disciples are troubled that Jesus is baptizing and "all are going to him." John's answer is pure joy: "A person cannot receive even one thing unless it is given him from heaven... The one who has the bride is the bridegroom. The friend of the bridegroom, who stands and hears him, rejoices greatly at the bridegroom's voice. Therefore this joy of mine is now complete. He must increase, but I must decrease." The friend of the bridegroom does not compete with the bridegroom. His joy is to hear the bridegroom's voice and step back. Every preacher, every ministry, every Hope Connection effort stands or falls by that sentence: He must increase; I must decrease. John's closing words rise to the heights: "The Father loves the Son and has given all things into his hand. Whoever believes in the Son has eternal life; whoever does not obey the Son shall not see life, but the wrath of God remains on him." Has, and remains — present tenses both. Eternal life is a present possession of the believer, and wrath is a present reality over unbelief. There is no third country between them.

Key verses

"Jesus answered him, 'Truly, truly, I say to you, unless one is born again he cannot see the kingdom of God.'" — John 3:3 (ESV)

"For God so loved the world, that he gave his only Son, that whoever believes in him should not perish but have eternal life." — John 3:16 (ESV)

"He must increase, but I must decrease." — John 3:30 (ESV)

"Whoever believes in the Son has eternal life; whoever does not obey the Son shall not see life, but the wrath of God remains on him." — John 3:36 (ESV)

The bad news and the good news

The bad news of John 3 is that no résumé saves — not religion, not office, not sincerity, not a lifetime of teaching others — because flesh gives birth to flesh, and people love the darkness rather than the light. But the good news is the most loved sentence ever spoken: God so loved this world that He gave His only Son, lifted up like the serpent in the wilderness, so that whoever believes in Him — the bitten who simply look — should not perish but have eternal life. The Son was not sent to condemn the world, but that the world should be saved through Him.

A note from the Reformers. Luther called John 3:16 "the gospel in miniature" — the whole of Scripture small enough to fit in one sentence and large enough to save the world. Faith Alone lives here: the promise is to "whoever believes," and believing is receiving, not achieving. If you memorize one verse in this Gospel, this is the one generations have chosen.

Reflection question: Nicodemus had religion, Scripture, and sincerity, and Jesus told him he needed a birth, not an improvement. What part of your old life are you still trying to polish when Jesus offers to make you new?
