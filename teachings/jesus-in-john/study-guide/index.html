A twenty-one-chapter reading plan for the Gospel of John, drawn from Jesus in John — A Chapter-by-Chapter Commentary. Use it on its own with just your Bible, or alongside the commentary.

"but these are written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name." — John 20:31 (ESV)

How to Use This Guide

The order of every session. First, read the chapter in your own Bible — all the way through, out loud if you can — before you read anything else. The guide serves the Scripture; it never replaces it. Then read the commentary section for that chapter if you have it, beginning with "Jesus in this chapter," so your eyes are fixed on Christ before anything else. Then sit with the chapter's question below. Answer it in prayer, out loud to God, and write your answer down.

Keep a notebook. A simple notebook is all you need: your answer to each chapter's question, the memory verse written out, and anything the Lord shows you along the way. A truth is not really yours until you have written it, prayed it, and lived it.

The rhythm: three sessions a week. One chapter per session, three sessions a week, and you will walk through all of John in seven weeks. If you want to go slower, spread one chapter across the whole week: read it one day, study it the next, and pray through the question on the third. Either pace is fine — what matters is the steadiness, not the speed.

No catch-up piles. If you miss a day, you do not owe anybody two chapters. Just pick up at the next chapter at the next session. Life happens — sickness, family, work, hard weeks. This rhythm bends and keeps going. The Lord is not keeping a tally, and neither are we.

Alone, together, or in a group. Alone: one Bible, one notebook, and prayer before and after. With a spouse or a friend: read the chapter aloud together, talk the question through honestly, and pray for each other out of what it showed you. In a small group: one chapter per meeting, read it aloud to open, save real time at the end for the question, and let the memory verse be the group's verse for the week.

Read in the Reformation way. Hold to the five watchwords of the Reformers as you go: sola scriptura — Scripture alone is the rule; sola gratia — grace alone saves; sola fide — faith alone receives it; solus Christus — Christ alone is the Savior; soli Deo gloria — glory to God alone. And in every chapter, keep the law and the gospel straight: the law shows us our sin and our need; the gospel shows us Christ and His finished work. John's Gospel will do the rest.

Study Questions — One per Chapter

Work each question in your notebook after you read the chapter. Many of them follow the Bible's own pattern of putting off the old and putting on the new (Ephesians 4:22–24; Colossians 3:8–10).

  1. John 1 — Receive the birth-gift. John says God's children are born of God, not of human will. Put off trying to manufacture your own new life; put on receiving it as a gift. Where have you been trying to build a new life by effort that Jesus offers to give you by new birth — and what would receiving look like this week?
  2. John 2 — Bring Him what has run dry. The wine ran out at Cana, and Jesus filled the lack with better than was lost. Put off hiding your emptiness and managing appearances; put on bringing your lack to Him plainly. What has run dry in your life — joy, strength, hope — that you will name to Him in prayer this week?
  3. John 3 — Stop polishing, start believing. Nicodemus had religion, Scripture, and sincerity, and Jesus told him he needed a birth, not an improvement. Put off polishing the old life; put on looking to the lifted-up Son. What part of your old life are you still trying to improve when Jesus offers to make you new?
  4. John 4 — Leave the jar. The woman left her water jar behind when she went to tell the town. Put off returning to the same old wells — the habits and comforts that never quench; put on drinking deeply of Christ and telling someone. What is your old well, and who is one person you could tell about the living water this week?
  5. John 5 — Do you want to be made well? Jesus asked a man sick for thirty-eight years if he wanted to be well. Put off settling into a brokenness you have stopped expecting Him to touch; put on hearing His word and rising. Name the long-standing place, and write down one step of obedience you will take at His word this week.
  6. John 6 — Love the Giver more than the gift. The crowd wanted bread that perishes and walked away from the Bread of Life. Put off seeking Jesus mainly for what He gives; put on coming to Him for who He is. What good gift of God have you been tempted to love more than the Giver — and how will you come to Him this week simply because He is enough?
  7. John 7 — Come and drink. Jesus cries His invitation to anyone who thirsts. Put off satisfying your thirst everywhere except at His feet; put on coming to Him daily and drinking. What thirst have you been carrying to other places, and what will a daily coming to Him look like this week?
  8. John 8 — Free indeed. The Son's freedom is real freedom, not a better-managed slavery. Put off managing a chain — hiding it, pacing it, making peace with it; put on bringing it to the Son to be broken outright. Name the chain, and name one person who will stand with you while you walk free of it.
  9. John 9 — Say your one thing. The healed man's whole theology fit in one sentence about what Jesus had done for him. Put off waiting until you can answer every question; put on simple witness. Write your "one thing I do know" in one sentence — and say it to one person this week.
  10. John 10 — Rest in His grip. Jesus says His sheep are held in His hand and the Father's. Put off trusting your grip on God; put on resting in His grip on you. When you feel yourself slipping, whose grip are you trusting? Write down John 10:28–29 and carry it with you this week.
  11. John 11 — Bring Him your "if only." Both sisters met Jesus with "Lord, if you had been here." Put off holding your grief at a distance from Him; put on bringing it to the One who wept at the grave Himself. Where do you need to know that the Resurrection is not only a future day but a Person standing in front of you now?
  12. John 12 — Let the grain fall. Jesus says fruit comes through a grain that falls and dies. Put off holding back the part of your life God is asking you to lay down; put on losing it for His sake and finding it multiplied. What are you holding back from the ground — and what is one step of letting go this week?
  13. John 13 — Wash feet without keeping score. Jesus washed the feet of men who would deny, scatter, and betray Him — and loved them to the end. Put off serving only those who deserve it or repay it; put on towel-and-basin love. Whose feet, literally or not, is He calling you to wash this week?
  14. John 14 — Take His road, not yours. Jesus says He is the way — not a way among ways. Put off trying to reach God, peace, or worth by a road of your own making; put on trusting the Person who is the road. Where are you still mapping your own way, and what would trusting Him there look like today?
  15. John 15 — Remain. Jesus' one command in this chapter is to abide. Put off the hurry and noise that pull you away from Him; put on staying close — His words in you, prayer, obedience. What pulls you away, and what would staying close look like this week, practically, one day at a time?
  16. John 16 — Take the peace He actually promised. Jesus promised trouble and peace in the same breath. Put off expecting a trouble-free life; put on the peace of the One who has overcome the world. Where do you need to stop demanding that the trouble leave before you will take His peace?
  17. John 17 — He prayed for you. Jesus prayed for everyone who would believe through the apostles' word — that includes you. Put off praying as if you were on your own; put on remembering you are prayed for by the Son Himself. What does it do to your heart to know He prayed for you by category before you were born — and how will you pray for another believer this week the way He prayed for you?
  18. John 18 — Stay by the right fire. Peter warmed himself at the wrong fire and denied the Lord in small sentences. Put off going quiet about Jesus where comfort and fear press you; put on standing with Him openly. Where do comfort and fear most tempt you to go quiet — and what is one plain sentence of witness you will not swallow this week?
  19. John 19 — Stop paying a paid debt. "It is finished" means nothing is left for you to pay. Put off trying to pay God back for what Christ finished; put on resting in the receipt. What debt do you keep trying to pay — guilt you rehearse, penance you perform — as if the receipt were not already stamped?
  20. John 20 — Believe without seeing. Jesus blessed those who have not seen and yet have believed — that is you, reading words on a page. Put off demanding a sign before you will trust; put on believing His word about the fear or doubt He is meeting today. Name the doubt, and write Thomas's confession under it: "My Lord and my God!"
  21. John 21 — Let Him restore you. Jesus restored Peter publicly, gently, and by name — three loves for three denials. Put off the belief that a failure has quietly disqualified you; put on following Him again and feeding His sheep. What failure do you believe has sidelined you, and what is the first small act of service He is handing back to you this week?

Christ Proclaimed. Jesus is Lord.
