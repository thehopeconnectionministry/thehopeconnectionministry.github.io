John 17 — This Is Eternal Life: The High Priestly Prayer

Jesus in John — A Chapter-by-Chapter Commentary

  • Teachings
  • Gospel of John
  • Commentary

Jesus in this chapter

In John 17, Jesus reveals Himself as the High Priest who prays for His own — for the men around Him, and then for everyone who will ever believe through their word — that they be kept in the Father's name, sanctified in the truth, and one, even as He and the Father are one. This is the true Lord's Prayer: the Son, on the night before the cross, letting us listen in.

Commentary

The hour has come; glorify your Son (17:1–5). "When Jesus had spoken these words, he lifted up his eyes to heaven, and said, 'Father, the hour has come; glorify your Son that the Son may glorify you.'" The hour that tolled "not yet" through the early chapters has come, and Jesus meets it in prayer. The glory He asks for is the cross and the crown together — glorify the Son in the lifting up, so that the Son may glorify the Father by finishing the work. And the work is defined by its gift: "since you have given him authority over all flesh, to give eternal life to all whom you have given him." Authority over all flesh — every human being who has ever lived is under His authority — exercised for one purpose: to give eternal life to the given ones. Then He defines eternal life Himself, and His definition is not length of days or a place on a map:

"And this is eternal life, that they know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent." — John 17:3 (ESV)

The word for know is ginōskō (γινώσκω, Greek) — to know by experience and relationship, the knowing of a shepherd and his sheep, not mere information about a subject. Eternal life, by Jesus' own definition, is this kind of knowing: of the only true God, and of Jesus Christ whom He sent. Heaven begins the moment that knowing begins, and it never ends because the Known never ends. Jesus reviews His work with the calm of completion before it is completed: "I glorified you on earth, having accomplished the work that you gave me to do. And now, Father, glorify me in your own presence with the glory that I had with you before the world existed." Before the world existed — the prayer reaches back behind chapter 1's beginning and forward past the cross in five verses. He prays from eternity, through the hour, into eternity.

I am praying for them (17:6–19). Now He turns to the men in the room — and to the shape of every believer's keeping. "I have manifested your name to the people whom you gave me out of the world. Yours they were, and you gave them to me, and they have kept your word." The disciples are the Father's gift to the Son — as the sheep of chapter 10 were given, so these men are given — and Jesus speaks of them generously: they have kept the word, they have believed He came from the Father. He knows about the scattering coming within hours, and He prays for them in the light of their calling, not their performance. "I am praying for them. I am not praying for the world but for those whom you have given me, for they are yours." This is particular, priestly prayer — the High Priest bearing specific names on His heart, as Aaron bore the names of the tribes on his breastpiece. His requests for them are few and enormous. First, keeping: "Holy Father, keep them in your name, which you have given me, that they may be one, even as we are one. While I was with them, I kept them in your name... I have guarded them, and not one of them has been lost except the son of destruction, that the Scripture might be fulfilled." He kept them while He was present; now He hands the keeping to the Father as He departs — and the keeping is in the name, in the revealed character of God, the safest address in the universe. Second, joy: "that they may have my joy fulfilled in themselves" — His joy, the joy of chapter 15, installed in frightened men. Third, protection in place: "I do not ask that you take them out of the world, but that you keep them from the evil one." Not evacuation — preservation. They are to stay in the world they are sent to, guarded from the evil one within it. "They are not of the world, just as I am not of the world." Fourth, sanctification: "Sanctify them in the truth; your word is truth. As you sent me into the world, so I have sent them into the world. And for their sake I consecrate myself, that they also may be sanctified in truth." The keeping of God's people is done by God's Word — holiness grows by truth, not by withdrawal — and the Priest consecrates Himself, sets Himself apart for the altar, so that His people may be set apart in truth. The cross is inside this prayer, consecrating the pray-ers by the Pray-er's sacrifice.

For those who will believe through their word (17:20–26). Then the prayer widens past the room, past the century, and lands on you: "I do not ask for these only, but also for those who will believe in me through their word." Everyone who has ever believed through the apostles' word — through this Gospel, through a sermon, through a grandmother's testimony, through a podcast heard in a truck at night — is named in that clause by category. Jesus prayed for you, specifically as one of those, on the night before He died for you. What does He ask for us? First, unity: "that they may all be one, just as you, Father, are in me, and I in you, that they also may be in us, so that the world may believe that you have sent me." The church's oneness is not organizational; it is a shared indwelling, modeled on the Trinity itself — and it is evangelistic: the world believes when it sees it. "The glory that you have given me I have given to them, that they may be one even as we are one, I in them and you in me, that they may become perfectly one, so that the world may know that you sent me and loved them even as you loved me." Read the end of that again: the Father loves the believers even as He loves the Son. That may be the most staggering clause in the prayer. Second, presence: "Father, I desire that they also, whom you have given me, may be with me where I am, to see my glory that you have given me because you loved me before the foundation of the world." His desire for heaven is that His people be with Him and see His glory — the prayer of chapter 14's promise, now prayed aloud. It will be answered; the One praying does not lose His petitions. He closes by contrasting the world's ignorance with the disciples' knowledge, and with a promise that is still unfolding: "O righteous Father, even though the world does not know you, I know you, and these know that you have sent me. I made known to them your name, and I will continue to make it known, that the love with which you have loved me may be in them, and I in them." I will continue to make it known — the risen Christ, by His Spirit, is still teaching His people the Father's name. And the final aim of all of it: the Father's love in them, and Christ in them. The prayer ends where the Gospel ends — life, in His name, inside His people.

There is a reason this chapter has changed more lives than can be counted, and I can say it plainly from my own. At my last stay at Haven of Rest — a men's discipleship program in South Carolina — after more than thirty years of addiction that rehabs and programs and my own will could not break, I was reading this chapter, and it was John 17:3 and John 17:20 that met me: eternal life is knowing God and Jesus Christ whom He sent — and Jesus, on the night before the cross, prayed for those who would believe through the apostles' word. That included me. I had been raised by my grandparents, and my grandmother took me to church, so the words were not new to my ears. But that day I believed, and God changed my life then and there. I tell it here, in this chapter and nowhere else in this volume, because this is where it belongs: my story is just one answer to this prayer. If He prayed for me, He prayed for you.

Key verses

"And this is eternal life, that they know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent." — John 17:3 (ESV)

"Sanctify them in the truth; your word is truth." — John 17:17 (ESV)

"I do not ask for these only, but also for those who will believe in me through their word." — John 17:20 (ESV)

"that the love with which you have loved me may be in them, and I in them." — John 17:26 (ESV)

The bad news and the good news

The bad news of John 17 is that the world does not know the Father, and that Jesus' own people, left to themselves, would scatter within hours and could never keep themselves. But the good news is that we are kept by a prayer: the Son has asked the Father to keep us in His name, sanctify us in His truth, make us one, and bring us to be with Him and see His glory — and the Father always hears the Son. Eternal life is knowing God, and the knowing was prayed for before it was ever felt.

A note from the Reformers. "Sanctify them in the truth. Your word is truth" (verse 17) — the keeping of God's people is done by God's Word, and the Reformers staked everything on it: Scripture Alone is not a slogan about a book but a confidence that the same Word that converted us will keep us. Jesus is still praying this prayer. It is still being answered.

Reflection question: Jesus prayed for everyone who would believe through the apostles' word — that includes you. What does it do to your heart to know He prayed for you, by category, the night before He died?
