This Season in Scripture — Ready Before the Sun Goes Down

Friday, with the light going early.

Around this season the sun sets early over the hill country, and by mid-afternoon every village from Hebron to Galilee is hurrying. Tomorrow's reading is Chayei Sara (Genesis 23–25), and it holds a quiet Friday-evening picture: “And Isaac went out to meditate in the field toward evening. And he lifted up his eyes and saw, and behold, there were camels coming” (Genesis 24:63, ESV).

Through their eyes, getting ready for Sabbath.

No cooking fire will be lit tomorrow, so bread is baked and stew is finished today, while it is still light. A mother fills and trims the lamps so they will burn into the evening, a father draws extra water and brings the animals in, and children are sent on last errands down muddy lanes — learning the rhythm of rest by helping make it ready. Travelers quicken their pace; no one wants to be caught on a wet road when the sun drops.

A quiet month, turning.

Cheshvan still has no feast of its own. Tradition keeps Sigd this Monday, 29 Cheshvan, and the next festival, Hanukkah, begins at sundown Friday, December 4 — 28 days away. For now, the preparation itself is the celebration.

Friend, God meets us in the getting-ready — in bread, lamps, and a field at evening, before the rest begins.
