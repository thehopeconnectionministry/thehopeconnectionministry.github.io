Acts 6 — Tables and the Word: Servants Chosen and the Word Multiplies

Jesus in Acts — A Chapter-by-Chapter Commentary

  • Teachings
  • Acts
  • Commentary

Jesus in this chapter

Jesus is the Lord of His church, and His word keeps growing as His people serve one another in small things. In Acts 6 the Lord turns an honest complaint into a harvest — not by silencing the overlooked, but by raising up servants. The chapter teaches us where Christ is found in the daily life of His church: in the word preached, in prayers prayed, and in food carried to widows whose names nobody else remembers.

Commentary

The complaint (6:1). Now in these days, when the disciples are increasing in number, a complaint arises. The Hellenists — Greek-speaking Jewish believers — murmur against the Hebrews because their widows are being neglected in the daily distribution of food. Growth brings friction, even in a Spirit-filled church. Luke does not hide this, and we should not be surprised by it: God's people can neglect God's people, and grumbling grows where needs go unnoticed. What matters is what the church does next. The complaint is not mocked, and the widows are not told to be more spiritual. The problem is treated as real, because to Christ it is real.

Servants chosen (6:2–6). The twelve summon the full number of the disciples and say something balanced and wise: "It is not right that we should give up preaching the word of God to serve tables." That is not contempt for tables. It is a division of labor — the apostles must devote themselves to prayer and to the ministry of the word, and the church must not let the tables go unserved either. So the congregation is told: "Pick out from among you seven men of good repute, full of the Spirit and of wisdom, whom we will appoint to this duty." The Greek word at the center is diakonia — service, ministry. Luke uses the same word for serving tables and for the ministry of the word. In Christ's church the tray and the pulpit carry the same word: ministry. The seven chosen are Stephen, Philip, Prochorus, Nicanor, Timon, Parmenas, and Nicolaus — all Greek names, most likely. The church answers a complaint from the Greek-speaking widows by trusting Greek-speaking men with the work. The apostles pray and lay their hands on them.

The word increases (6:7). Luke's summary verse is explosive, and it is worth reading slowly:

"And the word of God continued to increase, and the number of the disciples multiplied greatly in Jerusalem, and a great many of the priests became obedient to the faith." — Acts 6:7 (ESV)

Priests — men from the very establishment opposing the church — are coming to faith in numbers. And it happens in a chapter about a food distribution. Faithful order in small things and the advance of the word are not competitors. They never have been.

Stephen, full of grace and power (6:8–15). Stephen, chosen to wait tables, is soon "full of grace and power," doing great wonders and signs among the people. In this church, no service is small, and God promotes table-servants to pulpits. But opposition rises: men from the Synagogue of the Freedmen dispute with Stephen, and they cannot withstand the wisdom and the Spirit with which he speaks. So they resort to the old method — secretly instigated witnesses, false charges: "This man never ceases to speak words against this holy place and the law." He is seized and brought before the council, and the false witnesses set out their case. And all who sit in the council gaze at Stephen, "and they saw that his face was like the face of an angel." The man on trial is the calmest man in the room. Heaven is already shining on the witness the council is about to condemn.

Key verses

"But we will devote ourselves to prayer and to the ministry of the word." — Acts 6:4 (ESV)

"And the word of God continued to increase, and the number of the disciples multiplied greatly in Jerusalem, and a great many of the priests became obedient to the faith." — Acts 6:7 (ESV)

"And Stephen, full of grace and power, was doing great wonders and signs among the people." — Acts 6:8 (ESV)

The bad news and the good news

The bad news of Acts 6 is ordinary but real: God's people can neglect God's people, widows can be overlooked inside a growing church, and false witnesses can be found when honest argument fails. But the good news is how heaven answers — not by silencing the complaint but by raising up servants full of the Spirit and wisdom, until the word of God increases and even priests become obedient to the faith. The Lord of the church cares about the distribution list as much as the sermon, and He builds His kingdom through people willing to carry food to forgotten names.

A note from the Reformers. Calvin saw in this chapter the pattern for the church's offices in every age: the word, prayer, and care for the poor held together in one body, each honored, none neglected. A church that drops any one of the three, he would say, has stopped resembling Acts 6.

Reflection question: The apostles treated a food complaint as a spiritual matter worth solving well. Is there a small, unnoticed need around you — a name, a table, a task nobody thanks — that God may be calling you to meet this week?
