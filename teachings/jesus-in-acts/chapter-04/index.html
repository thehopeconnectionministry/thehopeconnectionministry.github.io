Acts 4 — No Other Name: Boldness Before the Council

Jesus in Acts — A Chapter-by-Chapter Commentary

  • Teachings
  • Acts
  • Commentary

Jesus in this chapter

Jesus is the rejected Stone whom God made the cornerstone, and the only name that saves. The risen Jesus gives His untrained witnesses boldness before the highest court in Israel — boldness they did not learn in a school but caught from being with Him. The council that condemned Jesus now faces His witnesses, and the confession they hear is the church's confession still: salvation in no one else.

Commentary

The arrest (4:1–4). Peter and John are still speaking when the priests, the captain of the temple, and the Sadducees come upon them — greatly annoyed because they are teaching the people and proclaiming in Jesus the resurrection from the dead. The Sadducees do not believe in resurrection at all, and a healed man standing in the temple is an argument they cannot answer, so they arrest the arguers. The apostles are put in custody until the next day. But the arrest comes too late for the opposition's purposes: many of those who heard the word believe, and the number of the men grows to about five thousand. Persecution and growth walk side by side in Acts. The word cannot be arrested.

Before the Sanhedrin (4:5–12). The next day the rulers, elders, scribes, Annas the high priest, Caiaphas, and the high-priestly family gather in Jerusalem — the same court, in many cases the same men, that condemned Jesus. The healed man stands among them, undeniable. "By what power or by what name did you do this?" Then Peter, filled with the Holy Spirit — Luke marks the filling, because what follows is not Peter's natural courage — gives the boldest answer in the book. If we are examined today about a good deed done to a crippled man, let it be known to all of you and to all the people of Israel:

"By the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, whom you crucified, whom God raised from the dead — by him this man is standing before you well. This Jesus is the stone that was rejected by you, the builders, which has become the cornerstone. And there is salvation in no one else, for there is no other name under heaven given among men by which we must be saved." — Acts 4:10–12 (ESV)

The Greek word for the boldness the council then observes is parrhēsia — plain, open speech with nothing held back. It is what the council "saw" in Peter and John, and what the church will pray for in verse 29: not safety, but boldness to keep speaking the word. And the stone saying is from Psalm 118: the builders examined this stone, rejected it, and God set it as the cornerstone of the whole building anyway. Human rejection does not have the last word over God's Christ.

Uneducated, common men — who had been with Jesus (4:13–22). Now when they see the boldness of Peter and John, and perceive that they are uneducated, common men, they are astonished. "And they recognized that they had been with Jesus." That sentence explains everything in Acts. Not trained, not credentialed — but with Jesus, and it shows. The council confers privately. The sign is notable and they cannot deny it; all the inhabitants of Jerusalem know it. So they do the only thing left to power that has lost the argument: they threaten. They charge them to speak no more in this name. Peter and John's answer becomes the church's answer in every age: "Whether it is right in the sight of God to listen to you rather than to God, you must judge, for we cannot but speak of what we have seen and heard." And the council, finding no way to punish them because of the people — for all are praising God for what has happened — lets them go.

The church prays for boldness (4:23–31). Released, the apostles go to their friends and report everything. And the church's response is not a strategy meeting; it is prayer — one of the great prayers of the New Testament. They lift their voices together to God and address Him as Sovereign Lord, who made the heaven and the earth and the sea and everything in them, and who spoke by the Holy Spirit through David in Psalm 2: the kings of the earth set themselves against the Lord and His Anointed — and did exactly what God's hand and plan had predestined to take place. Then comes the request, and it surprises us. They do not ask for the persecution to stop. They ask: "And now, Lord, look upon their threats and grant to your servants to continue to speak your word with all boldness, while you stretch out your hand to heal." And when they have prayed, the place is shaken, they are all filled with the Holy Spirit, and they continue to speak the word of God with boldness. Threatened people who pray like that cannot be silenced.

One heart and soul (4:32–37). The chapter closes with the church's inner life: the full number of those who believe are of one heart and soul, no one says that anything he has is his own, and there is not a needy person among them. Owners of lands and houses sell them and lay the proceeds at the apostles' feet, and distribution is made to each as any has need. Luke gives us one name to remember: Joseph, a Levite from Cyprus, called by the apostles Barnabas — "son of encouragement." We will meet him again; encouragement is about to become a load-bearing ministry in this book. Grace on the inside, boldness on the outside: that is the church of Acts 4.

Key verses

"And there is salvation in no one else, for there is no other name under heaven given among men by which we must be saved." — Acts 4:12 (ESV)

"But Peter and John answered them, 'Whether it is right in the sight of God to listen to you rather than to God, you must judge, for we cannot but speak of what we have seen and heard.'" — Acts 4:19–20 (ESV)

"And when they had prayed, the place in which they were gathered together was shaken, and they were all filled with the Holy Spirit and continued to speak the word of God with boldness." — Acts 4:31 (ESV)

The bad news and the good news

The bad news of Acts 4 is that the builders rejected the Stone — and we repeat their mistake every time we want a savior of our own design instead of the One God sent, or keep quiet about His name because speaking will cost us. But the good news is that God made the rejected Stone the cornerstone of the whole building, and His one name is wide open to everyone who calls on it. The same risen Jesus who gave untrained fishermen boldness before the Sanhedrin gives His people boldness still — and people can still tell when a person has been with Jesus.

A note from the Reformers. Spurgeon preached Acts 4:12 as the church's charter: no other name is not narrowness to be ashamed of but certainty to be grateful for — a drowning man does not complain that there is only one rope. Christ alone saves, and that one name is enough for the whole world.

Reflection question: The council could tell the apostles had been with Jesus. What would people be able to tell about you — and is there an area where you are keeping quiet about His name that you will speak in this week, kindly and plainly?
