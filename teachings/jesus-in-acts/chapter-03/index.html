Acts 3 — The Name That Heals: A Lame Man Walks and Sins Are Blotted Out

Jesus in Acts — A Chapter-by-Chapter Commentary

  • Teachings
  • Acts
  • Commentary

Jesus in this chapter

Jesus is present in Acts 3 by His name — and His name is enough to lift a lame man to his feet and to offer His own killers a blotted-out record. Peter is careful to give the glory where it belongs: it is not the apostles' power or piety that made this man walk, but faith in the name of Jesus. Refreshing comes from the presence of the Lord they crucified. The healer and the Savior are the same Person.

Commentary

At the Beautiful Gate (3:1–5). Peter and John are going up to the temple at the hour of prayer, the ninth hour. The apostles still pray in the temple courts — the new faith has not made them careless about the worship of God's house. At the gate called Beautiful, a man lame from birth is being carried in, laid daily at the gate to ask alms of those entering. He has been lame more than forty years (Acts 4:22). He has never taken a step. He sees Peter and John and asks for money — the only thing he has ever asked for, because it is the only thing he has ever imagined receiving. Peter directs his gaze: "Look at us." And he fixes his attention on them, expecting to receive something from them. He will receive something. It will not be what he expected.

Silver and gold I do not have (3:6–10). Then Peter says the sentence that has echoed through twenty centuries of ministry:

"But Peter said, 'I have no silver and gold, but what I do have I give to you. In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, rise up and walk!'" — Acts 3:6 (ESV)

Peter takes him by the right hand and raises him up, and immediately his feet and ankles are made strong. And leaping up, he stands and begins to walk, and enters the temple with them, walking and leaping and praising God. Isaiah had promised that in God's day "the lame man shall leap like a deer" (Isaiah 35:6) — and here it happens at the temple gate. All the people see him and are filled with wonder and amazement. Note what the church's wealth is. Not silver and gold. The name of Jesus, believed and spoken. A church rich in that name is never poor.

Peter's sermon in Solomon's Portico (3:11–18). While the man clings to Peter and John, the astonished crowd runs together to them. Peter sees it and addresses the people — and refuses the credit first: "Why do you stare at us, as though by our own power or piety we have made him walk?" The God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob has glorified His servant Jesus. Then Peter says the hard thing, to their faces, in God's own house: you delivered Him over, you denied the Holy and Righteous One in the presence of Pilate, you asked for a murderer to be granted to you, "and you killed the Author of life, whom God raised from the dead. To this we are witnesses." That is the bad news without a cushion. And yet even here Peter adds a mercy: "And now, brothers, I know that you acted in ignorance, as did also your rulers." Ignorance does not excuse the sin, but it opens the door to the sermon that follows.

Repent, and times of refreshing (3:19–26). Here is the turn — law giving way to gospel, as it always must:

"Repent therefore, and turn back, that your sins may be blotted out." — Acts 3:19 (ESV)

Blotted out — the record wiped clean, like ink washed from a page. The same Jesus they killed is the prophet like Moses whom God promised, the offspring of Abraham in whom all the families of the earth are blessed. And Peter ends with a sentence full of tenderness: "God, having raised up his servant, sent him to you first, to bless you by turning every one of you from your wickedness." Sent to you first — to the very city, the very people, who crucified Him. That is the order of grace. The blessing is not a payment; it is a turning — God blessing us by turning us.

Key verses

"And his name — by faith in his name — has made this man strong whom you see and know, and the faith that is through Jesus has given the man this perfect health in the presence of you all." — Acts 3:16 (ESV)

"Repent therefore, and turn back, that your sins may be blotted out." — Acts 3:19 (ESV)

"God, having raised up his servant, sent him to you first, to bless you by turning every one of you from your wickedness." — Acts 3:26 (ESV)

The bad news and the good news

The bad news of Acts 3 is Peter's plain charge: we denied the Holy and Righteous One and killed the Author of life, and every honest heart knows its own share in preferring lesser things to Christ. But the good news stands right beside it: the God who raised Jesus offers those very people a clean record — sins blotted out, times of refreshing from the presence of the Lord. The name that raised the lame man is mighty to raise dead sinners too, and He was sent to bless us by turning every one of us from our wickedness.

A note from the Reformers. Calvin remarked on Peter's refusal of credit in this chapter: the servants of Christ, he said, must always point away from themselves, because the power is in the name and not in the man. That is solus Christus with muddy shoes on — Christ alone does the saving; His servants do the pointing.

Reflection question: The lame man asked for coins and received healing. What small thing have you been asking God for, when He may want to give you something far deeper — and will you bring Him the deeper need honestly this week?
