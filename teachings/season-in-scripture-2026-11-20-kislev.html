This Season in Scripture — 10 Kislev 5787: The Road to the Square in the Month of Kislev

The road to the square.

Scripture dates it ahead of us, to the twentieth day of the ninth month, when the men of Judah and Benjamin gather in Jerusalem and “all the people sat in the open square before the house of God, trembling because of this matter and because of the heavy rain” (Ezra 10:9, ESV). Around this season, through their eyes, that day is coming closer: roads are wet, cloaks are heavy with rain, and a hard, honest reckoning waits in the cold — not hidden indoors, but out in the open before God.

Through their eyes, earning a living.

Shepherds lead their flocks down from the cold hill country toward the milder Jordan valley and the wilderness edges, where winter grass is showing green. A shepherd earns no wage in a warm room — his living is vigilance: counting sheep through the gate at dusk, mending a fold of stones and thorns, watching for jackals on the long nights.

The land itself.

Kislev rain fills cisterns that families will draw on for months, and it turns a dry wadi into a torrent by afternoon. Up in Judah's hills the wind cuts; down in the Jordan valley, a day's walk below, the air is almost spring-like. At dawn and dusk, households still speak the Shema (Deuteronomy 6:7).

What is coming.

Kislev has no feast commanded in Scripture — the quiet itself is the story. Ahead stands Hanukkah, beginning at sundown Friday, December 4 — 14 days away, on 25 Kislev. Households clean their lamps and set aside their best olive oil.

Friend, God still meets His people in the open, even in the rain — come to Him honestly today, and let Him make you clean and new.
