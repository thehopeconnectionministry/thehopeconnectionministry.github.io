This Season in Scripture — Isaac Sowed in That Land

Sowing, like Isaac.

This Shabbat’s reading is Toldot (Genesis 25:19–28:9), and it lands right in the sowing season: “And Isaac sowed in that land and reaped in the same year a hundredfold. The LORD blessed him” (Genesis 26:12, ESV). Around this season, from Gerar’s dry fringe to the Galilee hills, a farmer puts seed into rain-softened ground and cannot see one sprout. Isaac also reopens wells his father Abraham dug, stopped up by enemies (Genesis 26:18) — in that country, a well is life kept open.

Kislev, day one.

Today is 1 Kislev itself, the new moon’s second day — trumpets once sounded over its offerings (Numbers 28:11–15). Scripture dates a word to Zechariah to this month’s fourth day (Zechariah 7:1).

Through their eyes, on the lake.

While the hill country turns cold, the Sea of Galilee lies far below sea level and stays milder. In winter its warm springs at Tabgha draw fish inshore, so fishermen work the dark — hauling nets all night, washing and mending them at dawn (Luke 5:2), a son learning the knots beside his father. The catch is salted at Magdala; tonight many tables hold fish and barley bread.

What is coming.

Hanukkah begins at sundown Friday, December 4 — 23 days away, on 25 Kislev. It is kept at home, so households quietly check lamps and guard their new oil.

Friend, sow what God put in your hand, reopen the old wells of prayer, and trust Him with what you cannot yet see.
