## How to Use This Study Guide

This guide is the companion to *Jesus in Isaiah — A Chapter-by-Chapter Commentary*. Use it the same way the commentary teaches: **read the chapter in your own Bible first, then read the commentary, then work the questions.** One chapter per session, three sessions a week, will carry you through Isaiah in about twenty-two weeks. If you miss a day, do not pile up chapters — **no catch-up piles.** Pick up at the next chapter at the next session. The Lord is not keeping a tally, and neither are we.

For each chapter below you will find the theme, the key verse to watch for, one memory verse quoted in full from the ESV to hide in your heart, and — after the table — one study question per chapter to work through in a simple notebook, after the commentary's reflection question. A truth is not really yours until you have written it, prayed it, and lived it.

### Study Questions — One per Chapter

Work each question in your notebook after the chapter's reflection question. Many follow the Bible's own pattern of putting off the old and putting on the new (Ephesians 4:22–24; Colossians 3:8–10).

1. **Isaiah 1 — Come and reason.** Put off one untreated wound you have been covering with religious activity. Put on Isaiah 1:17: one act of justice or mercy toward someone powerless this week. Name the person.
2. **Isaiah 2 — Walk in the light.** Put off one exalted thing — a trust, a fear, a pride — that rivals the mountain of the LORD. Put on one step of walking in His light: a path from His teaching you have been postponing. Write it down with a day.
3. **Isaiah 3 — Props removed.** Put off leaning on one prop as if it were God. Put on Isaiah 3:15's question as your own audit: is anyone being crushed by your choices? Make one repair.
4. **Isaiah 4 — Washed by fire.** Put off asking God to shelter an unconfessed filth. Put on honesty: name the thing that needs His washing, out loud to Him, and receive the canopy of Isaiah 4:6 as a promise, not a feeling.
5. **Isaiah 5 — Fruit inspection.** Put off one stink-fruit habit — greed, drink, cynicism, moral confusion — from the six woes. Put on abiding: fifteen unhurried minutes in John 15 this week, asking the true Vine to do in you what the old vine could not.
6. **Isaiah 6 — Undone, cleansed, sent.** Put off serving God from an uncleansed conscience. Put on the order of Isaiah 6: confess until you say "Woe is me," receive the coal, then write one "Here am I, send me" step you will take this month.
7. **Isaiah 7 — Stand firm in faith.** Put off one self-salvation plan you are quietly funding. Put on Isaiah 7:9 as a daily sentence for one week: say it every morning about your biggest fear, by name.
8. **Isaiah 8 — Fear Him, not them.** Put off one conspiracy-fear that has been setting your mood. Put on Isaiah 8:20: one decision this week made by "the teaching and the testimony" instead of by the loudest voice. Record both.
9. **Isaiah 9 — Bring the need to the Name.** Put off carrying one burden under your own government. Put on the matching name of the Child — Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace — and pray that name over the burden daily for a week.
10. **Isaiah 10 — Lean in truth.** Put off leaning on the thing that struck you — the old alliance, the old coping. Put on remnant-faith: one concrete act of leaning on the mighty God "in truth," not in slogan.
11. **Isaiah 11 — Stump hope.** Put off pronouncing one cut-down area of your life dead. Put on stump-watching: write down the stump, and one small shoot of grace you can already see, however small.
12. **Isaiah 12 — Draw with joy.** Put off living on an old drink of grace. Put on a daily drawing: one verse from Isaiah 12 said aloud each morning this week, with thanks for anger turned away.
13. **Isaiah 13 — Babylon's lease.** Put off treating one proud system or habit as permanent. Put on the long view: write its name and Isaiah 13:11 over it, and refuse one participation in it this week.
14. **Isaiah 14 — Retire the five "I wills."** Put off one "I will ascend" ambition of the flesh. Put on refuge: say Isaiah 14:32 as your answer when the messengers ask what your hope is built on.
15. **Isaiah 15 — Weep like God.** Put off gloating or shrugging over one judged or fallen person. Put on God's heart for Moab: pray for that person by name three times this week, and do one kindness if a door opens.
16. **Isaiah 16 — Walk to the throne.** Put off one pride that keeps you wailing instead of bowing. Put on approach: come to the throne established in steadfast love (and Hebrews 4:16) with the exact request you have been too proud to bring.
17. **Isaiah 17 — Turn the gaze.** Put off looking to one altar of your own hands. Put on Isaiah 17:7: begin each day this week by literally saying, "Today I look to my Maker," and note what changes.
18. **Isaiah 18 — Honor the stillness.** Put off demanding that God perform on your timetable. Put on heat-of-sunshine faith: one area where you will work and wait quietly for thirty days without forcing an outcome.
19. **Isaiah 19 — Bless the enemy.** Put off one settled contempt for an "Egypt" or "Assyria" in your life. Put on Isaiah 19:25 as a prayer: ask God to make that person "my people... the work of my hands."
20. **Isaiah 20 — Barefoot obedience.** Put off one Egypt-flight you are planning. Put on Isaiah's kind of obedience: one costly, dignity-expensive act of faithfulness you have been avoiding. Do it this week.
21. **Isaiah 21 — Stay at the post.** Put off demanding a sunrise on schedule. Put on the watchman's answer: name your night honestly, affirm the morning, and return to your post — prayer, Word, fellowship — without quitting.
22. **Isaiah 22 — Hang on the Peg.** Put off tomb-carving: one place you are building your name. Put on vessel-life: transfer one specific weight from your shoulders to the Peg of Isaiah 22:23 — and to Jesus of Revelation 3:7 — in prayer, by name.
23. **Isaiah 23 — Consecrate the merchandise.** Put off assuming God cannot use your worst years' earnings — skills, experience, knowledge of the far country. Put on an offering: name one such thing and give it a holy use this month.
24. **Isaiah 24 — Start with the reign.** Put off reading the world's shaking as the final fact. Put on Isaiah 24:23: when the news frightens you this week, say first, "The LORD of hosts reigns on Mount Zion," and pray from there.
25. **Isaiah 25 — Wait at the table.** Put off one self-hosted feast — a comfort that cannot lift the veil. Put on waiting: set aside one meal this week to thank God in advance for the feast of Isaiah 25:6–8 and the tears He will wipe away.
26. **Isaiah 26 — Stay the mind.** Put off leaning your mind on the news, the symptom, or the balance. Put on *shalom shalom*: ten minutes daily this week with your mind deliberately stayed on Isaiah 26:3–4, read aloud.
27. **Isaiah 27 — Smash the altar stones.** Put off relating to God as an inspector to fear. Put on Keeper-trust — and take a hammer to one idol: a literal, physical act of removing one altar stone from your life this week.
28. **Isaiah 28 — Move your weight.** Put off one covenant with death — a lie you hide in. Put on the Cornerstone: name the lie, renounce it to God and one trusted person, and rehearse Isaiah 28:16 until panic loses its argument.
29. **Isaiah 29 — Unseal the book.** Put off lip-honor with a far heart in one habit of worship. Put on fresh joy for the meek: read one chapter of Isaiah aloud this week as if hearing it for the first time, and write what you actually heard.
30. **Isaiah 30 — Returning and rest.** Put off one swift-horse scheme. Put on quietness and trust: cancel one frantic effort, and take the saved time as literal rest before God — no phone, no fixing — this week.
31. **Isaiah 31 — Change the direction of the look.** Put off consulting one fleshly helper before God. Put on Isaiah 31:1's order: for your next real decision, consult the LORD first — in prayer and the Word — before any other counsel. Note the difference.
32. **Isaiah 32 — Take shelter.** Put off complacency about one guaranteed tomorrow. Put on the King's shelter: choose your needed image — hiding place, shelter, streams, shade — and pray from inside it daily this week.
33. **Isaiah 33 — Arm every morning.** Put off stockpiling anxiety as your treasure. Put on Isaiah 33:2 as a morning prayer for seven days, and Isaiah 33:22 as your confession whenever you need God to be one of His four offices.
34. **Isaiah 34 — Hand over the cause.** Put off nursing one Edom-grudge. Put on Romans 12:19 with Isaiah 34:8: write the cause down, pray it into God's day of vengeance, and do one act of release toward the person.
35. **Isaiah 35 — Walk the highway.** Put off believing your shut senses are permanent. Put on messenger-duty: say Isaiah 35:3–4 to one anxious-hearted person this week, in person, and mean it.
36. **Isaiah 36 — Silence at the wall.** Put off debating your Rabshakeh. Put on the king's command: identify the mocking voice's main argument, refuse it an answer, and set a time to carry it to God instead.
37. **Isaiah 37 — Spread the letter.** Put off re-reading your threatening letter alone. Put on Hezekiah's act: take the actual document, diagnosis, bill, or message, spread it before the LORD in prayer, and pray Isaiah 37:16–20 over it in your own words.
38. **Isaiah 38 — Turn to the wall.** Put off stoicism about one sentence you are living under. Put on Hezekiah's tears: pray honestly about it — and then write your own short testimony of one past bitterness God turned to welfare (Isaiah 38:17).
39. **Isaiah 39 — Show God, not treasure.** Put off one display of your assets, recovery, or knowledge. Put on testimony: the next time you speak of God's deliverance, name Him five times more than you name yourself. Count.
40. **Isaiah 40 — Wait for renewal.** Put off concluding that your way is hidden from God. Put on *qavah*-waiting: choose one promise from Isaiah 40 and wait on it deliberately — daily, aloud — for the length of this chapter's study.
41. **Isaiah 41 — Take the held hand.** Put off feeding one fear with arguments. Put on the five "I wills": write Isaiah 41:10 on a card, carry it a week, and answer the fear with the verse each time it speaks.
42. **Isaiah 42 — Let Him tend the wick.** Put off hiding your bruised, smoldering state from God. Put on the Servant's gentleness toward one other bruised reed: an act of patience with someone you would normally snap off or write off.
43. **Isaiah 43 — Hear your name.** Put off living as if your record introduces you to God. Put on Isaiah 43:1 and 43:25 together: say each morning, "He calls me by name; He will not remember my sins," and live one day as if both are fully true.
44. **Isaiah 44 — Ask the question.** Put off feeding on ashes. Put on Isaiah 44:20's question about one attachment — "Is there not a lie in my right hand?" — and act on the answer: return, because you have been redeemed.
45. **Isaiah 45 — Look and live.** Put off striving like clay against the Potter in one circumstance. Put on Spurgeon's simplicity: one honest look to Jesus about that circumstance, repeated every time the striving starts.
46. **Isaiah 46 — Be carried.** Put off hauling one burden-god — guilt, religion, self-salvation. Put on being carried: pray Isaiah 46:4 over your age, your weariness, and your future, and accept one act of help from God's people without apologizing for it.
47. **Isaiah 47 — Dethrone the "I am."** Put off one sentence of self-sufficiency ("I don't need..."). Put on Isaiah 47:4 as a confession said aloud: "Our Redeemer — the LORD of hosts is his name." Name one need to one person this week.
48. **Isaiah 48 — Go out from Babylon.** Put off trusting your refining as your standing. Put on name's-sake trust — and take one literal exit-step from your Babylon: a place, a habit, a subscription, a route you will no longer take.
49. **Isaiah 49 — Answer the feeling with the engraving.** Put off letting "forsaken and forgotten" go unchallenged. Put on Isaiah 49:15–16: every time the feeling comes this week, answer it aloud with the verse — and encourage one other "Zion" with it.
50. **Isaiah 50 — Kindle no torches.** Put off one self-lit torch — a scheme for light God has not given. Put on morning-by-morning teachability: fifteen minutes daily, ear first, mouth second, for the week of this chapter.
51. **Isaiah 51 — Look to the quarry.** Put off measuring God's future by your present smallness. Put on quarry-memory: write your Abraham-and-Sarah list — the small beginnings God has already multiplied in your life — and thank Him over it.
52. **Isaiah 52 — Run with the news.** Put off silence about the reign of God. Put on beautiful feet: carry Isaiah 52:7's headline — "Your God reigns" — to one person in darkness this week, in words they can hear.
53. **Isaiah 53 — Live at the exchange.** Put off carrying iniquity the Servant already bore. Put on Isaiah 53:5–6 as a daily confession and thanksgiving for one week — and memorize verse 5. If you do nothing else in this study, do this.
54. **Isaiah 54 — Enlarge the tent.** Put off measuring God's love by the brief moment. Put on tent-enlarging faith: prepare for one growth you cannot yet see — lengthen one cord, strengthen one stake — as an act of trust in Isaiah 54:10.
55. **Isaiah 55 — Bring your thirst.** Put off spending on one thing that is not bread. Put on the free market: redirect that money, time, or hunger toward the feast — and invite one thirsty person to come with you.
56. **Isaiah 56 — Gather, don't gatekeep.** Put off one way you have assumed the door was not for you — or not for someone else. Put on the Gatherer's work: welcome one outcast concretely this week, in Jesus' name.
57. **Isaiah 57 — Give God His address.** Put off presenting your improved self for God's approval. Put on contrition: confess one thing until it is pulverized — no softening words — and then receive Isaiah 57:18: "I have seen his ways, but I will heal him."
58. **Isaiah 58 — Loose one yoke.** Put off one religious performance that coexists with a burden you could lift. Put on the chosen fast: share bread, house, clothing, or freedom with one specific person this week — and watch for the dawn of verse 8.
59. **Isaiah 59 — Confess in the first person.** Put off "they" language about the culture's sins. Put on Isaiah 59's "we": write a confession of your own part — your separation, your silence — and then claim Isaiah 59:20–21 for your children and grandchildren by name.
60. **Isaiah 60 — Arise and shine.** Put off sitting in the dark as if the light had not come. Put on rising: one public, unhidden act of light this week — a kindness, a witness, a generosity — done because your Light has come.
61. **Isaiah 61 — Work the exchange.** Put off keeping your ashes as identity. Put on the Anointed's trade: bring ashes, mourning, or faintness to Him in prayer by name — then take up one rebuilding task among ruins, however small, as an oak of righteousness.
62. **Isaiah 62 — Give Him no rest.** Put off answering to an old name. Put on watchman-duty: choose one promise of God and pray it back to Him daily this week — take no rest, give Him no rest — and answer only to "Sought Out, A City Not Forsaken."
63. **Isaiah 63 — Recount the steadfast love.** Put off praying complaints-first. Put on Isaiah 63:7: before your next hard prayer, list aloud the steadfast loves of the LORD in your own history — then plead "You are our Father" over the present trouble.
64. **Isaiah 64 — Come as clay.** Put off bringing God a résumé. Put on Isaiah 64:6 and 64:8 together: confess the rags, claim the Potter, and ask Him — plainly, in prayer — to rend the heavens in one situation where improvement is not enough.
65. **Isaiah 65 — Leave the table of Fortune.** Put off one reliance on luck, fate, or omens — even joking ones. Put on Isaiah 65:24's confidence: pray one prayer this week expecting to be answered mid-sentence, and record what happens.
66. **Isaiah 66 — Tremble and worship.** Put off one way you have tried to house or manage God. Put on the three qualifications — humility, contrition, trembling at the Word — and close this study where the book closes: on your knees in worship, giving Him the last word.
