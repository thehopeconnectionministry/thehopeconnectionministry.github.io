This Season in Scripture — 6 Kislev 5787: The Potter's Hands in the Rainy Season

The potter's hands.

Around this season, when rain hammers the hills and the furrows are too wet to sow, work moves under a roof. Through their eyes: a potter sits at his wheel in a workshop, feet turning, hands wet with clay, shaping jars to hold oil, grain, and water. His son wedges clay beside him, learning the trade by touch. “But now, O LORD, you are our Father; we are the clay, and you are our potter; we are all the work of your hand” (Isaiah 64:8, ESV).

Through their eyes, earning a living.

A carpenter mends a plow and a yoke for the next dry spell; a smith sharpens tools; women weave wool indoors, grind barley, and stretch figs, lentils, and oil through winter. Children learn at the bench and the loom, not only at a scroll. At dawn and dusk the household speaks the Shema (Deuteronomy 6:7).

The land itself.

Hill-country roads run slick, wadis swell, and the coastal plain turns heavy with mud — a cart, not a footpath, decides who reaches market.

What is coming.

Hanukkah begins at sundown Friday, December 4 — 18 days away, on 25 Kislev. Households clean lamps and set aside their best new oil. Scripture dates a trembling assembly in Jerusalem to the twentieth day of this month, in the heavy rain (Ezra 10:9).

Friend, on the days the rain shuts one door, God still shapes a life with His hands — trust the Potter at your table today.
