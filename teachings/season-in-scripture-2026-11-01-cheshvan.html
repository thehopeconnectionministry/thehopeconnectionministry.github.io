This Season in Scripture — The Rain Prayer and the Fresh-Plowed Field

A quiet month, seen from the doorway

Cheshvan has no feast or fast of its own. The fall-feast pilgrims are home, the hill-country roads are emptying, and the next festival — Hanukkah, first candle lit Friday evening, December 4 — is still 33 days away. Nobody is packing for Jerusalem. They are filling oil jars, mending cloaks, watching the sky.

The rain they pray for

The daily prayer asking God for rain began on 7 Cheshvan — held back until the last pilgrims could cross the Jordan before the roads turned to mud. Four days ago, on 17 Cheshvan, came the date the Flood rains began (Genesis 7:11). In this same month, “in the month of Bul, which is the eighth month,” Solomon finished the Temple (1 Kings 6:38).

Through their eyes, at home

The early rain — the yoreh — softens ground baked hard all summer; only then can the plow bite. A farmer walks behind his oxen, his son beside him learning the furrow and the trade at once; barley goes in first, then wheat. In the courtyard, the last olives are pressed, fresh oil sharp in the cool air. Lamps are lit earlier, and the Shema is spoken morning and evening over it all: “He will give the rain for your land in its season, the early rain and the later rain, that you may gather in your grain and your wine and your oil” (Deuteronomy 11:14, ESV).

Friend, God does some of His best work in the quiet month — in furrows, oil jars, and ordinary prayers for rain.
