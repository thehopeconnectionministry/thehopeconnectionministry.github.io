This Season in Scripture — 8 Kislev 5787: The Empty Barn in the Month of Kislev

The empty barn.

Around this season, a hard word is on its way in this same month. Scripture dates it to the twenty-fourth day of the ninth month — 24 Kislev, still ahead of us — when God's word comes to Haggai over the Temple's foundation (Haggai 2:10, 18). Through their eyes: the builders stand in the cold with little to show, and the farmer asks, “Is the seed yet in the barn? Indeed, the vine, the fig tree, the pomegranate, and the olive tree have yielded nothing. But from this day on I will bless you” (Haggai 2:19, ESV). Nothing has sprouted yet. The promise comes before the proof.

Through their eyes, earning a living.

On the Gezer farmer's calendar these are sowing months. Between rains a farmer finishes sowing wheat, barley, and flax in soft ground, then repairs terraces and drains before the next downpour. Day laborers wait in the marketplace on wet mornings, hoping a landowner still needs hands — a day's wage, a denarius, is a family's bread (Matthew 20:2).

The land itself.

Jerusalem sits cold on her hills, lamps lit early and cloaks kept on indoors. Rain swells the wadis and turns the roads to mud, while down in the Jordan valley the air stays milder and the first green shows in the sown fields. At dawn and dusk, households speak the Shema (Deuteronomy 6:7).

What is coming.

Hanukkah begins at sundown Friday, December 4 — 16 days away, on 25 Kislev, the day after Haggai's dated word. Households clean their clay lamps and set aside good olive oil for the nights ahead.

Friend, if your barn looks empty today, hear the Lord in this season: His blessing is spoken before you can see it grow — trust Him in the waiting field.
