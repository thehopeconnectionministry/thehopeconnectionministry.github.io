This Season in Scripture — 18 Kislev 5787: A Sabbath Rest in Kislev — In the Month of Kislev

Saturday, November 28, 2026 · 18 Kislev 5787 · This Season in Scripture

In this season. Around this season, on a short Kislev Friday, work stops early. Scripture does not date the Sabbath to this one day — it comes every seventh day — but in Kislev the sun drops fast, and a family feels it. Through their eyes: bread is baked before noon, soup is kept warm without a fresh fire, lamps are filled and trimmed while there is still light, and cloaks are laid out for the cold walk home after dark. Then rest: "Six days you shall labor, and do all your work, but the seventh day is a Sabbath to the LORD your God" (Exodus 20:9–10, ESV).

Through their eyes, earning a living. This is a day with no earning — and that is the point. Yesterday the farmer rushed the last late sowing between rains, the potter banked his kiln, the merchant closed his stall, and day laborers were paid before sundown so no wage was carried unpaid over the Sabbath. Today hands are empty on purpose. Children who all week carried water, fed animals, and learned a trade beside their fathers and mothers learn something else today: how to be still, hear Torah read aloud, and memorize it by listening and repeating.

The land itself. Kislev rain can drum on flat roofs all night, turn village lanes to mud, and swell the wadis, while Hermon stays white in the north. Inside, a mud-brick house holds the warmth of yesterday's cooking, and the family gathers close around one good lamp.

What is coming. Hanukkah begins at sundown Friday, December 4 — 6 days away, on 25 Kislev. This week lamps get a deeper cleaning, wicks are cut fresh, and the best olive oil is set aside for light.

Friend, God built rest into the week before He built anything else into your schedule. Receive it today — He is not in a hurry, and neither are you.
