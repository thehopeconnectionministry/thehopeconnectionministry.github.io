This Season in Scripture — 14 Kislev 5787

Tradition places it here. Tradition places Reuben's birth on 14 Kislev. Scripture itself tells the story without the day: Leah, unloved and aching, bears Jacob's first son and names him from her hope — "The LORD has looked upon my affliction; for now my husband will love me" (Genesis 29:32, ESV). Through their eyes: a mother in a tent, holding a firstborn, daring to believe God has seen her.

Through their eyes, earning a living. Around this season on the Sea of Galilee, fishermen work between winter storms. When rain and wind keep boats ashore, fathers and sons sit in the courtyard mending nets, sorting torn mesh, and repairing sails — a boy's schooling is his father's hands. A good night's catch is salted or sold fresh at market; a stormy week means barley bread and patience.

The land itself. Kislev rain fills the Jordan a little more each week and turns Galilee's lanes to mud, while the valley below stays milder than Jerusalem's cold hills. Snow whitens Hermon to the north. Days are short, lamps are lit early, and families still pray for rain as they sow between showers.

What is coming. Kislev has no feast commanded in Scripture — the quiet itself is the story. Ahead stands Hanukkah, beginning at sundown Friday, December 4 — 10 days away, on 25 Kislev. Households clean their lamps and set aside their best olive oil.

Friend, God saw Leah in her affliction, and He sees you in this cold, quiet season too. Walk with Him today — He has not looked away.
