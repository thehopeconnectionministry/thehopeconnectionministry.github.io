This Season in Scripture — 16 Kislev 5787: The Face of a Brother — In the Month of Kislev

Thursday, November 26, 2026 · 16 Kislev 5787 · This Season in Scripture

In this season. Around this season the Torah reading is still Vayishlach — the morning after the wrestling. Scripture does not date it to this day; the reading falls here. Jacob lifts his eyes, sees Esau coming, bows to the ground, and runs into an embrace he did not expect: "To see your face is like seeing the face of God, and you have accepted me" (Genesis 33:10, ESV). Through their eyes: a limping father, children close behind him, learning that God had gone ahead of his fear.

Through their eyes, earning a living. In Kislev, Jacob settles briefly at Succoth in the Jordan valley and "built himself a house and made booths for his livestock" (Genesis 33:17, ESV). That is the season's work still: shepherds raising rough shelters against cold rain, farmers slipping out between showers to finish late sowing, and households pressing the last olives so oil is ready for lamps. Children learn by carrying, feeding, and watching beside their parents.

The land itself. The Jordan valley lies lower and milder than Jerusalem's hills — greener, less bitter at night — while rain keeps swelling the river and softening the roads up to Shechem. Hermon is white, daylight is short, and travel is measured in muddy miles, about a day's walk at a time.

What is coming. Hanukkah begins at sundown Friday, December 4 — 8 days away, on 25 Kislev. Families are cleaning lamps, trimming wicks, and setting aside their best olive oil for light in the darkest weeks.

Friend, the brother you fear may meet you with grace, because God goes before you. Walk with Him today — He is already on the road ahead.
