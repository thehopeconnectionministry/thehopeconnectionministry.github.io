All Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness.

2 Timothy 3:16 (ESV)

Why dig in the skimmed verses?

Every Bible reader has them: the chapters we speed through. The greeting lists. The genealogies. The building rosters and travel notes. They look like the fine print of Scripture — and we treat them that way.

But all Scripture is breathed out by God. When you really believe that, the “boring” verses start to jump off the page. A name in a greeting list turns out to be carved in stone in an ancient city. A two-verse interruption in a genealogy turns out to be a man named for pain whose prayer God stopped the list to answer. A foreign servant padding a rescue rope with old rags gets a personal promise from God that kings didn't get.

So we dug. The Hidden Gems Collection gathers these skimmed verses — each one explained, opened up, and carried home — and releases one gem a week, in order. Because there are no filler verses. Only un-dug ones.

The gems so far

Gem #1 — Erastus: The Name Carved in Stone (Romans 16:23)
A host, a city official, a scribe, and “the brother” — the sign-off is the sermon. The name everybody skims, found carved in a Corinth pavement since 1929.
