This Season in Scripture — Down to the Warm Valley

The flocks go down.

Around this season, shepherds lead their sheep down out of the cold hill country toward the warm Jordan valley. Nights around Bethlehem bite now, and the grass is thin; down near Jericho, far below sea level, the air stays mild and winter grazing is green. Through their eyes: a boy walks behind his father’s flock, staff in hand, learning the calls, the strays, and the safe folds — his schooling is the flock itself. God’s seasons roll on as He promised: “While the earth remains, seedtime and harvest, cold and heat, summer and winter, day and night, shall not cease” (Genesis 8:22, ESV).

Through their eyes, as the week begins.

Yesterday was rest; today work takes up again. In valley villages, mothers grind barley before light is full, daughters carry water jars from the spring, and merchants open their stalls in the cool. A day laborer earns a denarius — a day’s wage — if a landowner calls him. The Shema is spoken at dawn as at dusk (Deuteronomy 6:7).

The land itself.

From Jerusalem to Jericho is roughly fifteen to eighteen miles — one hard day’s walk, dropping from cold stone hills to warm valley floor, past wadis that can rush after rain.

What is coming.

Hanukkah begins at sundown Friday, December 4 — 19 days away, on 25 Kislev. Households still guard their new olive oil and check their lamps for the darkest nights of the year.

Friend, wherever this season moves you — hill or valley, cold or warm — the Shepherd goes down the road with His flock, and with you.
