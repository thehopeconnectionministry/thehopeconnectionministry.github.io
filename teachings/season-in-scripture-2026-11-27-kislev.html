This Season in Scripture — 17 Kislev 5787: Bad News in Kislev, Carried in Prayer — In the Month of Kislev

Friday, November 27, 2026 · 17 Kislev 5787 · This Season in Scripture

In this season. Scripture dates it to the month, not the day: "in the month of Kislev, in the twentieth year," Nehemiah is far from home in Susa when his brother arrives with news — Jerusalem's wall is broken down, its gates burned (Nehemiah 1:1–3). Through their eyes: a cupbearer in a king's palace, safe and fed, who hears about home and cannot eat. He weeps, fasts, and prays for days before he does anything else: "As soon as I heard these words I sat down and wept and mourned for days, and I continued fasting and praying before the God of heaven" (Nehemiah 1:4, ESV).

Through their eyes, earning a living. Around this season on the Sea of Galilee, fishermen work cold, rough water, then mend nets on shore while the wind decides if they go out again. In the villages, potters and smiths work indoors more, weavers finish heavy wool, and children learn the trade beside their parents — hands learning by doing, Torah learned by hearing the Shema morning and evening at home.

The land itself. Kislev rain swells the Jordan and turns the fords dangerous, mud slows the hill roads between Galilee and Judea, and Hermon stays white above it all. Lamps are lit early, oil is precious, and a traveler counts the miles in daylight, not wishes — roughly a day's walk at a time.

What is coming. Hanukkah begins at sundown Friday, December 4 — 7 days away, on 25 Kislev. Families are still cleaning lamps, trimming wicks, and setting aside good olive oil for light in the darkest weeks.

Friend, bad news carried to God in prayer is not wasted. Walk with Him today — He hears before He sends.
