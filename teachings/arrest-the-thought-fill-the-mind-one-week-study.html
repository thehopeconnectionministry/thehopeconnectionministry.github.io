One-Week Study — 2 Corinthians 10:5 & Philippians 4:8

Arrest the Thought, Fill the Mind — A One-Week Study

Seven days in the war for the mind: tear down the stronghold, then fill the ground with Christ.

  • 2 Corinthians
  • Philippians
  • The Mind
  • One-Week Study

We destroy arguments and every lofty opinion raised against the knowledge of God, and take every thought captive to obey Christ.

2 Corinthians 10:5 (ESV)

Built on 2 Corinthians 10:5 and Philippians 4:8 — the two movements of the mind at war: arrest the thought that opposes God, then fill the mind with Christ.

The week at a glance

  • Day 1 — The Battlefield: strongholds and spiritual weapons (2 Corinthians 10:3–5)
  • Day 2 — The Standard: the knowledge of God (John 17:17; Psalm 119:11)
  • Day 3 — The Exchange: anxiety for peace (Philippians 4:6–7)
  • Day 4 — The Filling, Part 1: true, honorable, just, pure (Philippians 4:8)
  • Day 5 — The Filling, Part 2: lovely, commendable, excellence, worthy of praise (Philippians 4:8)
  • Day 6 — The Person Behind the List: all eight in Christ (Hebrews 12:2; Colossians 3:1–2)
  • Day 7 — The Deep Dive: read "Arrest the Thought, Fill the Mind" in full, then the end-of-week reflection (below)

One day at a time. Miss a day, pick up the next one — no catch-up pile, no guilt. Set to begin Monday, November 23, 2026, the week Philippians 4:8 is the memory verse.

Day 1 — The Battlefield

Read: 2 Corinthians 10:3–5 Focus: The fight is real, but it is not fought with fleshly weapons. The strongholds are thoughts — arguments and lofty opinions raised against the knowledge of God. A stronghold is a lie believed so long it feels like a fact. One question: What is one stronghold-thought that has dug in over the years — and can you say it plainly, in one sentence?

Day 2 — The Standard

Read: John 17:17; Psalm 119:11 Focus: Every thought is measured against one standard: what God has said. "Your word is truth." The Word stored in the heart is the weapon already in the house when the fight comes — you cannot arrest a lie with a verse you do not have. One question: Which verse do you actually have stored up right now that answers your stronghold from Day 1?

Day 3 — The Exchange

Read: Philippians 4:6–7 Focus: Before Paul ever lists what to think on, he gives the trade: anxiety out, prayer in — and the peace of God stands guard over heart and mind in Christ Jesus. The guard is posted by prayer. The mind does its work under His protection, not alone. One question: What is the specific anxiety you will trade in prayer today — named, not general?

Day 4 — The Filling, Part 1

Read: Philippians 4:8 (first four qualities) Focus: TRUE — what matches reality as God sees it. HONORABLE — what carries weight and dignity. JUST — what is right and fair by God's standard. PURE — what could be brought into the light without shame. The arrested thought leaves ground behind; these four are the first stones of the rebuilding. One question: Of the four, which replacement thought do you most need today — and what is it, in your own words?

Day 5 — The Filling, Part 2

Read: Philippians 4:8 (last four qualities) Focus: LOVELY — what calls forth love and softens the heart. COMMENDABLE — what would be a credit if said out loud. EXCELLENCE — not barely-allowed, but the best. WORTHY OF PRAISE — what God Himself would applaud. The standard rises from "not sinful" to "worthy of Him." One question: What have you been dwelling on lately that passes "not sinful" but fails "excellent"?

Day 6 — The Person Behind the List

Read: Hebrews 12:2; Colossians 3:1–2 Focus: The eight qualities are not eight chores — they are one Christ, seen from eight sides. He is the Truth. He is just and pure. He is altogether lovely. "Think about these things" finally means fix your eyes on Him and set your mind on things above, where He is. One question: When you picture fixing your eyes on Jesus (Hebrews 12:2), which of the eight qualities do you see in Him most clearly — and why that one?

Day 7 — The Deep Dive and the Week's Reflection

Read: "Arrest the Thought, Fill the Mind" (the full deep-dive study), slowly, with a pen. Then sit with the reflection below. Read each question, answer it in your own words first, then read the answer given.

End of Week — Reflect: Questions & Answers

Q1: What is a stronghold, and what does God say to do with it? A: A stronghold is a thought dug in against the knowledge of God — a lie believed so long it feels like a fact. It is not to be negotiated with or managed; by the weapons God gives, it is destroyed, and every thought is taken captive to obey Christ (2 Corinthians 10:4–5).

Q2: Why must the arrested thought be replaced, and not just removed? A: Because a mind is never empty. Paul pairs the two movements on purpose: arrest what opposes God (2 Corinthians 10:5), then dwell on what is true, honorable, just, pure, lovely, commendable, excellent, and worthy of praise (Philippians 4:8). Victory is replacement — Christ in the place the lie stood.

Q3: Where does peace come from in this fight — my effort, or somewhere else? A: From God, through prayer. "Do not be anxious about anything, but in everything by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known to God. And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and your minds in Christ Jesus" (Philippians 4:6–7). I bring the requests; He posts the guard.

Q4: What standard decides if a thought may stay? A: The knowledge of God — what He has said in His Word, which is truth (John 17:17). Feelings do not get a vote; circumstances do not get the final word. A thought that agrees with God's Word may stay. A thought that opposes it is a captive to take.

Q5: Who is the filling, finally — a list, or a Person? A: A Person. All eight qualities are perfectly true of Jesus Christ: He is the Truth (John 14:6), the Righteous One (1 John 2:1), unstained and pure (Hebrews 7:26), altogether lovely (Song of Solomon 5:16). To think on these things is to fix your eyes on Him (Hebrews 12:2) — and the same hands that were nailed to the cross are the hands holding the mind that trusts Him.

Closing prayer for the week: Lord, teach me the whole motion — to arrest every thought that rises against Your Word, and to fill my mind with Your Son. Guard my heart and my mind in Christ Jesus, and let what I dwell on this week make me more like Him. Amen.
