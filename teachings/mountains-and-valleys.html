The Mountains and Valleys of the Bible

He is God of the hills. He is God of the valleys. And He is not finished.

  • Mountains and Valleys
  • Bible Teaching
  • 72 Stories
  • The Hope Connection

"Because the Syrians have said, 'The LORD is a god of the hills but he is not a god of the valleys,' therefore I will give all this great multitude into your hand, and you shall know that I am the LORD."

1 Kings 20:28 (ESV)

There is a lie as old as the hills themselves, and Israel's enemies told it out loud. In 1 Kings 20:28, the Syrians said of Israel's God: "The LORD is a god of the hills but he is not a god of the valleys." They believed God had a territory — that He was strong on the high ground and absent down low. God answered that lie Himself, and He answered it in a valley, with a victory nobody could explain any other way: "Because the Syrians have said, 'The LORD is a god of the hills but he is not a god of the valleys,' therefore I will give all this great multitude into your hand, and you shall know that I am the LORD."

This series exists to bury that lie under the weight of the whole Bible.

From Genesis to Revelation, God keeps His appointments on mountains and in valleys. On the mountains, He reveals who He is — His holiness on Sinai, His provision on Moriah, His glory on the Mount of Transfiguration. In the valleys, He proves that He stays — with David in the valley of the shadow, with Ezekiel among the dry bones, with a weeping king crossing the Kidron, and with His own Son in a garden at the valley's edge. The mountain gives you a vision worth carrying. The valley gives you a testimony worth telling. And the God of the Bible refuses to be only one of the two.

In this series we will walk every mountain and every valley the Bible names — seventy-two stories in all:

  • Part I — The Mountains of the Old Testament
  • Part II — The Mountains of the New Testament
  • Part III — Mountains of Prophecy: What Is Still to Come
  • Part IV — The Valleys of the Old Testament
  • Part V — The Valleys of the New Testament
  • Part VI — Valleys of Prophecy: What Is Still to Come

Each story carries the same three questions: What happened there? What does it mean? And what does it point to? Because none of these places is trivia. Every one of them is a signpost — pointing to Christ, pointing to the church, pointing to the day when every valley shall be lifted up and every mountain made low, and pointing, finally, to you: where you have been, where you are standing right now, and where God is taking you.

Men climb Everest to prove something about themselves. Scripture tells a better story: a God who came down the mountain, walked through the valley, and met us where we were.
