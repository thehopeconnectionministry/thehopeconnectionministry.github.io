# Jesus in Psalms — Study Guide

**The Hope Connection**  
**Lewis Fuller**

A five-book reading plan for the Psalter, drawn from *Jesus in Psalms — A Commentary on the Psalter*. Use it on its own with just your Bible, or alongside the commentary.

*"And he said to them, 'These are my words that I spoke to you while I was still with you, that everything written about me in the Law of Moses and the Prophets and the Psalms must be fulfilled.'"* — Luke 24:44 (ESV)

---

## How to Use This Guide

**The order of every session.** First, read the psalm (or the small run of psalms) in your own Bible — all the way through, out loud if you can, praying it as you go — before you read anything else. The guide serves the Scripture; it never replaces it. Then read the commentary entry for that psalm if you have it, beginning with "Jesus in this psalm," so your eyes are fixed on Christ before anything else. Then sit with the question for that part of the Psalter below. Answer it in prayer, out loud to God, and write your answer down.

**Keep a notebook.** A simple notebook is all you need: the memory verses written out, your answers to the questions, and anything the Lord shows you along the way. A truth is not really yours until you have written it, prayed it, and lived it.

**The rhythm: three sessions a week.** This fits the study rhythm used across The Hope Connection: three sessions a week, one book at a time, until the book is done. With the Psalms, a natural session is one of the great psalms with its full commentary treatment, or a small run of shorter entries — three to five psalms at a sitting. At that pace you will walk the whole Psalter in the better part of a year. If you prefer one psalm a day with your morning coffee, that is a fine and ancient way to pray this book. Either pace is fine — what matters is the steadiness, not the speed.

**No catch-up piles.** If you miss a day, you do not owe anybody two sessions. Just pick up at the next psalm at the next session. Life happens — sickness, family, work, hard weeks. This rhythm bends and keeps going. The Lord is not keeping a tally, and neither are we. The Psalms are full of people who failed, wandered, and came back — and found the door still open.

**Alone, together, or in a group.** Alone: one Bible, one notebook, and prayer before and after — and on the days you have no words of your own, pray the psalm itself, slowly, in Jesus' name. With a spouse or a friend: read the psalm aloud together, talk the question through honestly, and pray for each other out of what it showed you. In a small group: take one great psalm per meeting, read it aloud to open, save real time at the end for the question, and let the memory verse be the group's verse for the week.

**Read in the Reformation way.** Hold to the five watchwords of the Reformers as you go: *sola scriptura* — Scripture alone is the rule; *sola gratia* — grace alone saves; *sola fide* — faith alone receives it; *solus Christus* — Christ alone is the Savior; *soli Deo gloria* — glory to God alone. And in every psalm, keep the law and the gospel straight: the law shows us our sin and our need; the gospel shows us Christ and His finished work. Calvin called this book "an anatomy of all the parts of the soul" — whatever you find in yours as you read, you will find a psalm that has already carried it to God, and a Savior who has already prayed it.

## Study Questions — One per Key Psalm

Work each question in your notebook as you reach that psalm. Many of them follow the Bible's own pattern of putting off the old and putting on the new (Ephesians 4:22–24; Colossians 3:8–10).

### Book One

1. **Psalm 1 — Move your roots.** Put off one counsel-of-the-wicked input you have been walking in — a voice, a feed, a habit of company. Put on meditation: one passage, morning and night, for a week. Which passage, and at what two times of day?
2. **Psalm 2 — Kiss the Son.** Where are you still resisting Christ's lordship — one area where His rule feels like cords? Put off the resistance; put on surrender, concretely: what is one act of obedience in that exact area this week?
3. **Psalm 8 — Receive your worth.** Put off proving your significance. Put on Psalm 8's order: wonder at God's majesty first, then at His mindfulness of you. Spend ten minutes outside under the sky this week and pray verses 1–4 aloud. What did the smallness teach you?
4. **Psalm 16 — Choose your portion.** Name the thing you are treating as your portion — whose loss would feel like losing everything. Put it down on paper, and write beside it: "The LORD is my chosen portion and my cup." Pray verse 5 over your actual circumstances until you can add, "The lines have fallen for me in pleasant places."
5. **Psalm 19 — Let the Word search you.** Put off one hidden fault the Word has been showing you; put on confession by name, and the closing prayer of the psalm every morning this week: "Let the words of my mouth and the meditation of my heart be acceptable in your sight."
6. **Psalm 22 — Pray the whole psalm.** When you feel forsaken, do not stop at verse 1. Read the entire psalm aloud, slowly, and mark where the turn comes. Then say verse 24 over your own affliction as a statement of fact: "He has not hidden his face from me, but has heard, when I cried to him." What affliction will you carry through the whole psalm this week?
7. **Psalm 23 — Say "my."** Which line of the psalm is your life arguing with — "I shall not want," "I will fear no evil," or "goodness and mercy shall follow me"? Put off arguing; put on praying that line back to the Shepherd daily until it settles. Write down any way He answers.
8. **Psalm 27 — Narrow to one thing.** Write down your current "one thing" as your worries reveal it. Then write David's. Put off one fear-driven habit this week; put on one act of seeking God's face — a set time, a set place, His presence the agenda.
9. **Psalm 32 — End the silence.** What have you been keeping silent about before God? Put off covering; put on David's sentence, spoken aloud with the thing named: "I will confess my transgressions to the LORD." Then read the verdict as yours: "and you forgave the iniquity of my sin."
10. **Psalm 40 — Testify.** Put off hiding your rescue. Put on Psalm 107's assignment early: tell one person this week how the LORD drew you up — the pit, the rock, the new song. Who is the person, and when will you tell them?

### Book Two

11. **Psalm 42 — Preach to your soul.** Write down what your soul has been saying to you. Then write the refrain as your reply. Put off listening passively to your despair; put on cross-examining it — "Why are you cast down?" — and commanding it: "Hope in God; for I shall again praise him." Do it out loud, daily, for a week.
12. **Psalm 45 — Forget your father's house.** What room in the old life are you still keeping? Put off the old loyalty; put on the procession — one step this week that publicly belongs to the King: a confession of faith, a baptism step, a telling of your story, an act of obedience someone can see.
13. **Psalm 46 — Be still on purpose.** Put off one noise source for a set period each day this week. Put on stillness with a text: read Psalm 46 aloud, slowly, and sit in silence for five minutes after verse 10 — "Be still, and know that I am God." What does the stillness uncover?
14. **Psalm 51 — Come clean completely.** Pray the whole psalm aloud with your own sin named where David named his — no soft words for hard sins. Put off the year of silence; put on verse 13: teach transgressors. Who is one person your restoration could instruct, and how will you reach them?
15. **Psalm 69 — Hand over the vengeance.** Name one reproach or wrong you have been carrying and rehearsing. Put off executing your own justice; put on Psalm 69's method: tell God about it in detail, in prayer, and leave the sentence with Him. End by praying verse 33 for someone else who is a prisoner — literal or otherwise.
16. **Psalm 72 — Stand where the King stands.** The King's reign is measured by what happens to the poor and needy. Put off one comfort that insulates you; put on one concrete act this week — a cause defended, a need met, a person treated as precious — that places you where your King stands.

### Book Three

17. **Psalm 73 — Take your envy into the sanctuary.** Whose prosperity has been preaching to you? Put off the sideways glance and the desk-bound rumination; put on worship — bring that exact envy to God in the assembly, with the people of God, and ask for the sanctuary's sight: their end, your portion. Write down what changes.
18. **Psalm 84 — Dig a well in Baca.** Name your current valley of weeping. Put off merely surviving it; put on well-digging: one prayer, one testimony, one act of praise performed in the valley itself, for the pilgrim coming behind you. Who might drink from it?
19. **Psalm 89 — File the plea.** Write one promise of God and one ruin of yours side by side, Ethan-style. Pray the question honestly — "Where is your steadfast love of old?" — and then close, as the book closes, with the doxology anyway: "Blessed be the LORD forever! Amen and Amen." Keep the page. God keeps His oaths; date yours.

### Book Four

20. **Psalm 90 — Number one day.** Put off the assumption of unlimited time. Put on Moses' arithmetic for a single day: steadfast love in the morning before anything else, every hour received as counted, and your work consciously handed to God to establish before you begin it. What did the numbering change?
21. **Psalm 91 — Move into the shelter.** Where does your soul actually dwell — what does it return to by default? Put off one default of fear; put on the first ten minutes of the morning in Psalm 91, saying verse 2 aloud as your address: "My refuge and my fortress, my God, in whom I trust." And check yourself: are you anywhere using God's promises as a dare instead of a dwelling?
22. **Psalm 95 — Answer today.** Where is your Meribah — the place your heart keeps asking, "Is the LORD among us or not?" Put off testing; put on one act of soft-hearted obedience today, before the day ends. Write down what it was, and what the voice said.
23. **Psalm 102 — Pour it out before the LORD.** What complaint have you poured out everywhere except to God? Put it in words to Him — all of it, unedited — and then stay in the prayer until you can say the turn: "But you, O LORD, are enthroned forever." What is your "but you" tonight?
24. **Psalm 103 — Make the list.** Forget not: write your own benefits list — forgiveness, healings, redemptions from pits, crowns, satisfactions — with names and dates. Put off forgetfulness; put on blessing the LORD aloud from your list, daily, for a week. Then say verse 12 over your worst sin, slowly, until the distance registers.

### Book Five

25. **Psalm 110 — Offer yourself freely.** Are you among the volunteers, or still negotiating terms with the King? Put off one negotiated condition — one "if You will, then I will." Put on a free offering: one act of service, giving, or obedience this week done for no other reason than that He reigns and intercedes for you.
26. **Psalm 118 — Rejoice in the day He made.** Put off trusting in princes — name the human refuge you have been leaning on hardest. Put on refuge in the LORD and the psalm's discipline: begin each day this week by saying verse 24 aloud before you look at anything else. "This is the day that the LORD has made." What is He making of it?
27. **Psalm 119 — Take the next step by lamplight.** What step has the Word already lit that you have not taken? Put off waiting for a floodlight on the whole road; put on obedience to the light you have. Name the step, and take it this week.
28. **Psalm 121 — Hand over the keeping.** Choose one journey, appointment, or night of this week and hand it to the Keeper beforehand, out loud: "The LORD will keep my going out and my coming in." Put off self-keeping — the rehearsing, the bracing, the 3 a.m. guard duty. He neither slumbers nor sleeps; you are allowed to do both.
29. **Psalm 130 — Wait on the word.** Name the iniquities that would sink you if God marked them. Then say verses 4 and 8 over them by name: "with you there is forgiveness… he will redeem Israel from all his iniquities." Put off waiting on your feelings; put on waiting on His word — more than watchmen for the morning. Which promise will you post on the wall?
30. **Psalm 139 — Invite the search.** Pray verses 23–24 slowly, and then be quiet and let Him answer. Put off hiding — there is nowhere to hide, and in Christ there is no need. Put on confession of the one grievous way He shows you, and let Him lead you in the way everlasting. Write down what He showed you, and what you did with it.
