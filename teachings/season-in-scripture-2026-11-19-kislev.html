This Season in Scripture — 9 Kislev 5787: The Scroll in the Fire in the Month of Kislev

The scroll in the fire.

Scripture dates it to the ninth month: in Kislev, a fast is proclaimed in Jerusalem, and Baruch reads Jeremiah’s scroll aloud in the Temple courts (Jeremiah 36:9–10). Through their eyes: the king sits in his winter house, a fire burning in the brazier before him, and as each column is read he cuts it off with a knife and throws it into the flames (Jeremiah 36:22–23). But the bad news will not burn away. God tells Jeremiah, “Take another scroll and write on it all the former words” (Jeremiah 36:28, ESV).

Through their eyes, earning a living.

This is a scribe’s world: Baruch earns his bread by the pen — ink mixed from soot and gum, a reed brush, a scroll copied word by word, for a careless copy is a corrupted word. On wet days when the late sowing rests, fathers teach their children the same way: hearing the Torah spoken and repeating it back, line upon line, until the words live in the mouth (Deuteronomy 6:7).

The land itself.

The ninth month brings the year’s shortest days to the hills of Judah. By mid-afternoon the light is failing over Jerusalem and lamps are trimmed early. Rain softens the roads between villages, and a traveler counts his miles carefully — a wadi crossed dry at dawn can run deep by evening.

What is coming.

Kislev carries no feast commanded in Scripture — the quiet itself is the story. Ahead stands Hanukkah, beginning at sundown Friday, December 4 — 15 days away, on 25 Kislev. Households already clean their lamps and guard their best olive oil for those nights.

Friend, take heart in the dark month: a king can burn a scroll, but he cannot burn God’s word. He writes it again — and it stands.
