This Season in Scripture — 15 Kislev 5787: Ten Days to Light, a Night at the Jabbok — In the Month of Kislev

Wednesday, November 25, 2026 · 15 Kislev 5787 · This Season in Scripture

In this season. Around this season the Torah reading is Vayishlach — Jacob coming home after twenty years, sending messengers ahead to Esau and hearing he approaches with four hundred men. Scripture does not date it to this day; the reading falls here. Through their eyes: a father dividing his camp in the dark, praying, "I am not worthy of the least of all the deeds of steadfast love and all the faithfulness that you have shown to your servant" (Genesis 32:10, ESV) — then wrestling alone at the Jabbok until dawn.

Through their eyes, earning a living. Kislev is late sowing on the Gezer calendar. Between rains, farmers slip back into softened fields to finish wheat and barley and sow flax, while indoors mothers and daughters weave heavier wool for winter cloaks. A son learns the trade at his father's side; a daughter learns the loom and the household from her mother — school is hands beside hands.

The land itself. Rain swells the Jordan and its crossings, like the Jabbok ford, and turns the hill roads from Galilee to Judea slick and slow. Hermon stays white, Jerusalem is cold, and darkness comes early over mud-brick homes.

What is coming. Hanukkah begins at sundown Friday, December 4 — 9 days away, on 25 Kislev. Families clean lamps, trim wicks, and set aside their best olive oil, preparing light in the darkest weeks.

Friend, Jacob limped into sunrise still held by God. Walk with Him today — He meets us on the road home, too.
