This Season in Scripture — 13 Kislev 5787: A Shepherd’s Cold Morning in the Month of Kislev

Nehemiah’s burden came in this month.

Scripture dates it by the month, not the day: “Now it happened in the month of Chislev, in the twentieth year, as I was in Susa the citadel,” Nehemiah hears that Jerusalem’s walls lie broken (Nehemiah 1:1, ESV). Through their eyes: a man far from home, serving in a foreign court, weeps and prays over a ruined city he has never stopped loving — in the cold, quiet month when the fields themselves seem to wait.

Through their eyes, earning a living.

Around this season, shepherds in the hills near Bethlehem move their flocks to lower ground before the worst cold. A boy walks beside his father, learning which wadi floods first and how to lift a lamb out of the mud — his schooling is the hillside. At night they fold the sheep into a stone pen, count them in one by one, and sleep across the opening. A lost sheep in Kislev rain is not a small thing; it is bread off the family’s table.

The land itself.

The early rains have softened Judah’s hills and the first green shows on the terraces. Late sowing continues between showers — wheat and barley still going in, flax in the small plots — while dry streambeds can turn to torrents in an hour. Jerusalem sits cold in the wind; a day’s walk north or south, the roads are mud, and travelers plan around the weather, not against it.

What is coming.

Kislev has no feast commanded in Scripture — the quiet itself is the story. Ahead stands Hanukkah, beginning at sundown Friday, December 4 — 11 days away, on 25 Kislev. Households clean their lamps and set aside their best olive oil.

Friend, if this season feels cold and quiet, you are in good company — Nehemiah prayed in Kislev, and God was already moving. Walk with Him in the ordinary cold, and trust Him with the walls you cannot rebuild alone.
