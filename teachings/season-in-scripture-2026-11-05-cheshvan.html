This Season in Scripture — Kindness at the Well in the Rainy Season

At the well at evening

In this season synagogues read Chayei Sara (Genesis 23–25) for Shabbat — Abraham's servant, sent north from the Hebron hills, meets Rebekah at a well. Around this season the scene feels close: early rains are filling cisterns from Galilee to Judea, yet water is still drawn jar by jar, and tired travelers and camels depend on a stranger's kindness. Rebekah does more than offer a drink: “I will draw water for your camels also, until they have finished drinking” (Genesis 24:19, ESV) — bucket after bucket, at dusk, unhurried.

A quiet month, looking ahead

Cheshvan still has no major feast of its own. Ethiopian Jews mark Sigd on 29 Cheshvan, this coming Monday, and the next great festival, Hanukkah, begins at sundown Friday, December 4 — 29 days away. No one travels yet; households simply keep storing oil for winter lamps.

Through their eyes, at the loom

As evenings lengthen, a mother weaves a heavier wool cloak while her daughter learns the loom beside her, thread by thread — a trade learned by doing. On the Sea of Galilee, fishermen mend nets against sudden rainy-season squalls, and at home the Shema is spoken as lamps are lit early against the dark.

Friend, God often meets us the way He met a servant at a well — through ordinary kindness, offered fully, in an ordinary season.
