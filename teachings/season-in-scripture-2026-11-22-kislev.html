This Season in Scripture — 12 Kislev 5787: Back to the Loom on the First Day in the Month of Kislev

Back to work on the first day.

Around this season, the week begins again at sunset's end. Through their eyes: the Sabbath lamp has burned out, the courtyard is cold, and hands return to work while the sky is still pale. A father heads to a field too wet to plow, a mother sets the loom, and the prayer goes with them: “establish the work of our hands upon us; yes, establish the work of our hands!” (Psalm 90:17, ESV).

Through their eyes, earning a living.

On rainy Kislev days, the weaver earns her bread indoors. Wool from the spring shearing, washed and spun, is stretched on a wooden loom; her daughter sits beside her, learning to throw the shuttle and beat the threads tight — a trade and an upbringing in one lesson. A finished cloak keeps out the hill-country cold and can be sold at market. Beside her, a husband mends a plowshare and sows flax in a small plot between rains.

The land itself.

From the coastal plain to Judah's hills, the sown fields are turning green in patches. The roads are soft, the wadis run after rain, and Hermon stands white in the clear air to the north. At dawn and dusk, households still speak the Shema (Deuteronomy 6:7).

What is coming.

Kislev has no feast commanded in Scripture — the quiet itself is the story. Ahead stands Hanukkah, beginning at sundown Friday, December 4 — 12 days away, on 25 Kislev. Households clean their lamps and set aside their best olive oil.

Friend, as your week begins, put your hands back to honest work and trust God to establish it — He is with you in the ordinary field and at the ordinary loom.
