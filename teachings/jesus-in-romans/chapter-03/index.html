Romans 3 — No One Righteous, and the Righteousness of God

Jesus in Romans — A Chapter-by-Chapter Commentary

  • Teachings
  • Epistle to the Romans
  • Commentary

Jesus in this chapter

Jesus is the answer this chapter has been waiting three chapters to give. He is the propitiation — the mercy seat — whom God put forward in His blood (3:25). He is the Redeemer in whom justification comes as a gift through redemption (3:24). He is the ground on which God can be both just and the justifier of the one who has faith in Him (3:26). The first two chapters and a half show Christ on the horizon as Judge; here He steps into the center as the sacrifice who satisfies the judgment. Everything in Romans before verse 21 is a courtroom. Everything from verse 21 is a gospel.

Commentary

The Jew's advantage, and God's faithfulness (3:1–8). Paul has just leveled the privileges of his people, and an objector asks the obvious question: "Then what advantage has the Jew? Or what is the value of circumcision?" Paul answers honestly: "Much in every way. To begin with, the Jews were entrusted with the oracles of God" (3:1–2). Israel held the Scriptures themselves — no small trust. But did Israel's unbelief cancel God's faithfulness? "By no means! Let God be true though every one were a liar" (3:3–4). God's character does not rise and fall with man's response; He remains true when every witness against Him is a liar. Then Paul handles a slander twisted out of his own gospel — the charge that he teaches "let us do evil that good may come" — and dismisses it the way it deserves: "Their condemnation is just" (3:8). Grace is not a license, and the man who hears it as one has not heard it at all.

The charge: all under sin (3:9–18). Now the prosecution rests its case, and Paul states the conclusion of chapters 1–3 in one sentence: "For we have already charged that all, both Jews and Greeks, are under sin" (3:9). Under sin — under its power, its guilt, its dominion, the way a people live under a occupying army. Then Paul strings together a chain of Old Testament quotations, a catena of Scripture, so that the verdict comes not from an apostle's opinion but from God's own Word:

"None is righteous, no, not one; no one understands; no one seeks for God. All have turned aside; together they have become worthless; no one does good, not even one." — Romans 3:10–12 (ESV)

The chain runs through the whole man: the throat an open grave, tongues practicing deceit, lips with the venom of asps, mouths full of curses and bitterness, feet swift to shed blood, ruin and misery in their paths, and the way of peace unknown — and the root of it all in the last line: "There is no fear of God before their eyes" (3:18). Speech, conduct, character, and finally the heart that does not fear God. This is not Paul being harsh. This is Scripture being honest, and it is honest about all — Jew and Greek, pagan and moralist, the man in the gutter and the man in the pew.

Every mouth stopped (3:19–20). "Now we know that whatever the law says it speaks to those who are under the law, so that every mouth may be stopped, and the whole world may be held accountable to God" (3:19). Every mouth stopped — no more excuses, no more "but I," no more self-defense. The courtroom goes silent. And then the law's true office is named: "For by works of the law no human being will be justified in his sight, since through the law comes knowledge of sin" (3:20). The law was never a ladder. It is a mirror and an X-ray: it shows the disease in perfect detail and cures none of it. Three chapters of bad news end here, at the bottom, with the whole world accountable and every mouth shut. That silence is the most hopeful sound in the letter so far — because into that silence, God is about to speak.

But now: the righteousness of God (3:21–26). These six verses are the heart of Romans and arguably the heart of the Bible. Handle them slowly.

"But now the righteousness of God has been manifested apart from the law, although the Law and the Prophets bear witness to it — the righteousness of God through faith in Jesus Christ for all who believe." — Romans 3:21–22 (ESV)

But now. After the long night of chapters 1–3, the sun comes up in two words. The righteousness of God — the gift-righteousness of chapter 1's theme — has been manifested, put on display, apart from the law: not by law-keeping, not earned, not mixed with our record. And yet not against the Old Testament: the Law and the Prophets bear witness to it. It comes through faith in Jesus Christ for all who believe — "for there is no distinction: for all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God, and are justified by his grace as a gift, through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus" (3:22–24). All have sinned — the level ground of guilt. Justified — declared righteous, a courtroom verdict of acquittal. By his grace as a gift — free, unearned, unbought. Through the redemption — the slave-market word: a price paid to buy a captive out. And then the deepest word in the chapter:

"whom God put forward as a propitiation by his blood, to be received by faith." — Romans 3:25 (ESV)

The Greek word is hilastērion — the word the Greek Old Testament used for the mercy seat, the golden lid of the ark of the covenant where the high priest sprinkled blood on the Day of Atonement (Leviticus 16). Paul is saying that God put Jesus forward, publicly, as the true mercy seat: the place where God's wrath against sin is satisfied and God's mercy meets the sinner. Propitiation means God's righteous anger against sin is not ignored, waved away, or suppressed — it is answered, in full, by the blood of His own Son. And this was necessary to show God's righteousness: "because in his divine forbearance he had passed over former sins" (3:25). For centuries God had passed over sins — forgiven David, spared generations — and a watching universe could ask whether He took sin seriously at all. The cross is His answer. "It was to show his righteousness at the present time, so that he might be just and the justifier of the one who has faith in Jesus" (3:26). Just, and the justifier. He does not clear the guilty by lowering the standard; He clears the guilty by bearing the sentence. The Judge remains righteous, and the sinner who believes goes free, and both are true at the same cross.

Boasting excluded (3:27–31). The conclusion falls like a hammer on human pride: "Then what becomes of our boasting? It is excluded. By what kind of law? By a law of works? No, but by the law of faith. For we hold that one is justified by faith apart from works of the law" (3:27–28). There is the Reformation in one verse: sola fide, faith alone — not faith plus, not faith after, not faith eventually, but faith apart from works of the law. And this one God is God of the Gentiles too — He will justify the circumcised by faith and the uncircumcised through faith, one door for all mankind. Does faith then overthrow the law? "By no means! On the contrary, we uphold the law" (3:31) — faith establishes the law in its true place, as the holy standard Christ fulfilled and the guide the justified now love. Chapter 4 will prove all of it from the life of one man: Abraham.

Key verses

"None is righteous, no, not one; no one understands; no one seeks for God." — Romans 3:10–11 (ESV)

"so that every mouth may be stopped, and the whole world may be held accountable to God." — Romans 3:19 (ESV)

"But now the righteousness of God has been manifested apart from the law, although the Law and the Prophets bear witness to it — the righteousness of God through faith in Jesus Christ for all who believe." — Romans 3:21–22 (ESV)

"for all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God, and are justified by his grace as a gift, through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus, whom God put forward as a propitiation by his blood, to be received by faith." — Romans 3:23–25 (ESV)

"For we hold that one is justified by faith apart from works of the law." — Romans 3:28 (ESV)

The bad news and the good news

The bad news of Romans 3 is total: none is righteous, no, not one; no one seeks for God; every mouth is stopped and the whole world is held accountable. The law gives knowledge of sin and nothing more — it cannot justify a single human being. If the chapter ended at verse 20, it would end in silence and despair. But the good news is the greatest turn in all of Scripture: But now. God has manifested His righteousness apart from the law. He put His own Son forward as the propitiation — the mercy seat sprinkled with blood — so that He might be just and the justifier of the one who has faith in Jesus. All have sinned, and sinners are justified by His grace as a gift. Not sold, not loaned, not earned in installments — a gift, received by faith alone. Boasting is excluded forever, because there is nothing left to boast in except the cross.

A note from the Reformers. Luther called Romans 3:28 the chief article of the whole Christian faith, and the Reformers staked everything on its plain words: justified by faith apart from works of the law. When the church of his day told Luther that faith must be completed by works before God could accept a man, he answered with this verse — the sinner's faith does not complete anything; it receives a justification already complete in Christ.

Reflection question: "Every mouth stopped" means no self-defense before God. Is there an excuse you are still quietly holding onto for a known sin — and will you lay it down today and take justification the only way it is given: as a gift, by faith alone?
