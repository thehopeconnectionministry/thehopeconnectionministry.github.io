Hebrews 3 — Greater Than Moses: Today, If You Hear His Voice

Jesus in Hebrews — A Chapter-by-Chapter Commentary

  • Teachings
  • Hebrews
  • Commentary

Jesus in this chapter

Greater than Moses, the Son over the house warns and woos in the same breath — Today is still open, and the voice that a generation resisted now melts the hearts that hold fast to Him. Jesus is revealed here as the Apostle and High Priest of our confession, faithful over God's house as a Son where Moses was faithful in it as a servant, and as the living voice of Psalm 95 — the God who still speaks today, calling His people to hold fast and to exhort one another daily so that none is hardened by the deceitfulness of sin.

Commentary

Consider Jesus, the Son over the house (3:1–6). "Therefore, holy brothers, you who share in a heavenly calling, consider Jesus, the apostle and high priest of our confession" (3:1). Consider — fix your mind on Him, the way you would study something you cannot afford to misunderstand. He is called Apostle — the One sent from God — and High Priest — the One who brings us to God; the two offices meet in one Person. Then comes the comparison the readers' hearts were aching over. Moses was the greatest name in their history. The preacher honors him fully: Jesus "was faithful to him who appointed him, just as Moses also was faithful in all God's house" (3:2). But the comparison is not close. "For Jesus has been counted worthy of more glory than Moses — as much more glory as the builder of a house has more honor than the house itself" (3:3). Moses was part of the furniture of God's house, a servant in it. Christ built the house. "For Moses was faithful in all God's house as a servant, to testify to the things that were to be spoken later, but Christ is faithful over God's house as a son" (3:5–6). Servant in the house; Son over the house. And then the preacher turns it toward us with a condition that is also a comfort: "And we are his house, if indeed we hold fast our confidence and our boasting in our hope" (3:6). Holding fast is not how we earn a place in the house. It is how the household shows itself — the family resemblance of the Son's own people.

Psalm 95 and the wasted generation (3:7–11). Now the sermon quotes the Holy Spirit's own words — note that the preacher attributes Psalm 95 to the Spirit Himself (3:7) — and the quotation is the hinge of two chapters:

"Therefore, as the Holy Spirit says, 'Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts as in the rebellion, on the day of testing in the wilderness,'" — Hebrews 3:7–8 (ESV)

The story behind it is the saddest in the Old Testament. The generation that came out of Egypt heard God's voice with their own ears, watched His works for forty years — the sea split, the manna fell, the water flowed — and still hardened their hearts at Meribah, still refused at Kadesh to enter the land. "They always go astray in their heart; they have not known my ways" (3:10). So God swore in His wrath, "They shall not enter my rest" (3:11). Hearing is not the same as believing. A whole generation proved that a person can be surrounded by miracles for forty years and die unbelieving in the desert. And a Today can be wasted — that is the bad news this chapter will not let us dodge.

Take care — and exhort one another daily (3:12–19). The application is direct, communal, and urgent: "Take care, brothers, lest there be in any of you an evil, unbelieving heart, leading you to fall away from the living God" (3:12). Falling away begins in the heart — an unbelieving heart — before it ever shows in the feet. And the God-given antidote is not solitary willpower: "But exhort one another every day, as long as it is called 'today,' that none of you may be hardened by the deceitfulness of sin" (3:13). Sin deceives — it never presents itself honestly; it promises life and delivers hardening. The defense is a church that will not let each other drift in silence: daily, mutual, honest exhortation. "For we have come to share in Christ, if indeed we hold our original confidence firm to the end" (3:14). Holding firm is the evidence of sharing in Christ — not the price of it, but the proof of it, the way fruit proves a living root. The preacher then walks the wilderness story once more, slowly, so no one misses it: Who heard and rebelled? "Was it not all those who left Egypt led by Moses?" With whom was God provoked forty years? "Was it not with those who sinned, whose bodies fell in the wilderness?" (3:16–17). Corpses in the sand — that is where unbelief ends. "So we see that they were unable to enter because of unbelief" (3:19). Not because the land was too strong, not because God was too weak — because of unbelief. The door was open. They would not walk through it believing.

"We are his house" — and the daily means (3:6, 13–14). Notice how carefully the preacher states the household promise: "we are his house, if indeed we hold fast our confidence and our boasting in our hope." The holding fast does not build the house; Christ the Son built it and rules it. Holding fast is how the household is known — the way smoke shows a living fire in the chimney. And God has not left the holding to solitary grit. His ordained means sits in verse 13: "exhort one another every day, as long as it is called 'today.'" Daily — not weekly, not occasionally. One another — mutual, not one-directional; the exhorter today is the exhorted tomorrow. The wilderness generation wandered as a crowd that grumbled together and hardened together; the new-covenant people are to be a company that speaks faith to one another until none is hardened by sin's deceit. A believer trying to hold fast alone is a believer refusing one of God's own keeping instruments. The voice that says "today" comes to us, most weeks, through the mouth of a brother or sister. Let it.

Hardness is a process, not an event (3:8, 13). Notice the grammar of the warning: "do not harden your hearts" — and behind it, the deceitfulness of sin doing the hardening by degrees. No one in the wilderness woke up one morning and chose apostasy outright. They grumbled about water. They tired of manna. They believed the spies' report instead of Caleb's. Small refusals, repeated daily, calloused a whole generation — the way a laborer's hands harden by small frictions, until they cannot feel what they once felt. That is why the preacher's antidote is also daily and also small: exhort one another every day. Softening is a process too. A conscience kept tender by daily hearing, daily confession, and daily encouragement from God's people does not harden in a night. The battle of chapter 3 is won or lost at the level of days — which is why the chapter's great word is not "someday" or "at the end," but today, repeated like a drumbeat: today, if you hear His voice.

Moses himself points past himself (3:5). One phrase in the comparison is easy to read past and worth stopping for: Moses was faithful "as a servant, to testify to the things that were to be spoken later." Moses' own ministry, in the preacher's reading, was a testimony aimed forward — the law, the tabernacle, the Passover, and the prophet-like-Moses promise all testifying to a later speaking, the speaking God would do by His Son. To honor Moses most truly is therefore to follow where Moses pointed. The readers tempted to go back to Moses as a final resting place were, ironically, disobeying Moses, whose whole service consisted in bearing witness to the things spoken later. The servant is honored when the household listens to the Son the servant announced. That is true well beyond this letter: every faithful minister, every sermon, every book — this one included — is a servant testifying to things spoken in the Son. When the testimony is done, the servant steps aside, and the house listens to its Lord.

Key verses

"Therefore, holy brothers, you who share in a heavenly calling, consider Jesus, the apostle and high priest of our confession," — Hebrews 3:1 (ESV)

"but Christ is faithful over God's house as a son. And we are his house, if indeed we hold fast our confidence and our boasting in our hope." — Hebrews 3:6 (ESV)

"But exhort one another every day, as long as it is called 'today,' that none of you may be hardened by the deceitfulness of sin." — Hebrews 3:13 (ESV)

"So we see that they were unable to enter because of unbelief." — Hebrews 3:19 (ESV)

The bad news and the good news

The bad news of Hebrews 3 is that a whole generation heard God's voice with their own ears, watched His works for forty years, and still hardened their hearts in the wilderness until God swore they would not enter His rest. Hearing is not the same as believing, and a Today can be wasted — sin deceives, and an unbelieving heart can grow inside a person surrounded by God's mercies. But the good news is that the invitation is still standing open while it is called Today: "Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts." The same voice that a generation resisted melts the hearts that hold fast to the Son — and we are His house, a household He keeps by sending us one another, daily, to exhort and be exhorted, so that none of us drifts in silence.

A note from the Reformers. Calvin heard in this chapter the church's marching orders for mutual care: the command to exhort one another daily means no believer is a private project. Spurgeon preached "today" as the sinner's only safe word — tomorrow is the devil's word, he said; God's word is today. Both men took the warning at full strength, and both pointed trembling hearers to the Son over the house, who holds His household fast.

Reflection question: The wilderness generation heard God's voice for forty years and hardened anyway — what is He saying to you today, and what would holding fast, concretely, look like before the day is over?
