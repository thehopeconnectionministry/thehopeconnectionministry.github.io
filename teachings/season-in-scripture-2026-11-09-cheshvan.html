This Season in Scripture — On a High Hill, Facing Jerusalem

Tradition keeps Sigd today.

Fifty days after the Day of Atonement, Beta Israel families in Ethiopia fast, climb the nearest high hill at dawn, and turn their faces toward Jerusalem. Their leaders, the kessim, carry the Orit — the Torah — up the slope, read from Exodus, Deuteronomy, and Nehemiah, and lead the people in confessing their sins and renewing the covenant. Through their eyes: a city most have never seen is still home, if you face it together and bow low.

That bowing is the heart of it.

Sigd means prostration — and it has an old picture: “And Ezra opened the book in the sight of all the people… And they bowed their heads and worshiped the LORD with their faces to the ground” (Nehemiah 8:5–6, ESV).

Through their eyes, as Cheshvan closes.

Around this season in the hill country, this is the quiet month’s last day. A potter shapes small oil lamps in his courtyard and sets them to dry, a mother counts sealed jars of new oil and barley in the store corner, and children help carry in dried figs — learning the household’s provision by handling it. Tonight Rosh Chodesh Kislev begins; Hanukkah follows at sundown Friday, December 4 — 25 days away — and those lamps will matter in the dark.

Friend, God hears a people who bow low and face home — and He meets us, too, when we turn our faces toward Him again.
