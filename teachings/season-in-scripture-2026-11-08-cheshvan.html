This Season in Scripture — The Week Begins — and Eyes Turn Toward Jerusalem

The first morning of the week.

Sabbath rest is over, and around this season a hill-country village stirs before light. In the marketplace, day laborers stand waiting to be hired — a denarius, a day’s wage, if a landowner calls their name. The potter sits back down at his wheel, the weaver at her loom, and farmers return to fields the early rain has softened, still sowing barley and wheat. Over it all a family prays the old prayer: “Let the favor of the Lord our God be upon us, and establish the work of our hands upon us” (Psalm 90:17, ESV).

Tonight, eyes turn toward Jerusalem.

At sundown begins Sigd — tradition keeps it tomorrow, 29 Cheshvan, fifty days after the Day of Atonement. In the Beta Israel villages of Ethiopia, families fast, climb the nearest high hill, and face toward Jerusalem, a city most have never seen, renewing the covenant as in Nehemiah’s day. Through their eyes: longing is itself a kind of worship.

A quiet month, filling with oil.

Cheshvan still has no feast of its own, and the next festival, Hanukkah, begins at sundown Friday, December 4 — 26 days away. For now the presses finish their work and the new oil is sealed in jars — the same glad oil that will soon light winter lamps.

Friend, God establishes ordinary work, and He hears a longing heart — even from a far hill, facing home.
