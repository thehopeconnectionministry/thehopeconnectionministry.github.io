This Season in Scripture — 11 Kislev 5787: Sabbath Rest in the Month of Kislev

Sabbath in the short days.

Around this season, the week turns toward rest early. Through their eyes: by Friday afternoon the sowing stops, the market empties, bread is baked and lamps are filled, and a family walks home in the cold light. “Remember the Sabbath day, to keep it holy” (Exodus 20:8, ESV). In Kislev’s darkness, rest is trust spoken at the table, with the Shema at dawn and dusk (Deuteronomy 6:7).

Through their eyes, earning a living.

On Galilee’s shore, fishermen fish at night and mend by day — washing linen nets so they will not rot, patching tears while their sons learn the trade beside them. A good catch is salted and sold inland; a stormy night means empty nets and a lean table. On the Sabbath, even the boats rest on the beach.

The land itself.

The Sea of Galilee sits far below the hills, milder than cold Jerusalem, but winter winds rush through the gaps and can whip the lake white in an hour. Northward, Hermon whitens; southward, the Jordan runs fuller with the rains.

What is coming.

Kislev has no feast commanded in Scripture — the quiet itself is the story. Ahead stands Hanukkah, beginning at sundown Friday, December 4 — 13 days away, on 25 Kislev. Households clean their lamps and set aside their best olive oil.

Friend, rest today the way they did — stop, trust God with the field and the nets, and let His rest hold your house.
