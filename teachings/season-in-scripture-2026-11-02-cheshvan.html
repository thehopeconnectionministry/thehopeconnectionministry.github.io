This Season in Scripture — The Shepherds Come Down From the Hills

Down from the cold hills

In Cheshvan the high pastures of Judea and Galilee turn wet and cold, and a shepherd knows not to stay up there. He brings his flock down to sheltered valleys nearer the village — where a boy learns the work beside his father, staff in hand, watching for the lame and straying. Snow can whiten Hermon to the north while the Jordan valley, far below, stays warm and green.

A quiet month, still

Cheshvan has no feast of its own. This week synagogues read Chayei Sara — Sarah's burial in the cave of Machpelah (Genesis 23). The next festival, Hanukkah, begins at sundown Friday, December 4 — 32 days away. No one travels yet; they prepare slowly. Oil pressed from the last olives is stored carefully, jar by jar — the same oil a household needs for winter lamps.

Through their eyes, at the well

At dawn, women and girls carry jars to the well and cistern, the clay cold against the hip. Cisterns cut in summer are filling again as the early rains run off roofs and courtyards. At dusk the flock is counted in, a simple reed flute sounds from a doorway, cloaks are pulled tighter, and the shepherd trusts this promise: “He will tend his flock like a shepherd; he will gather the lambs in his arms” (Isaiah 40:11, ESV).

Friend, the Good Shepherd still brings His own down from the cold hills, counts them in, and carries the weak home.
