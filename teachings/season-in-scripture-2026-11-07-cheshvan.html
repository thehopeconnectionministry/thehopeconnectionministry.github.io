This Season in Scripture — Dry Ground at Last

Scripture dates it to this very day.

“In the second month, on the twenty-seventh day of the month, the earth had dried out” (Genesis 8:14, ESV). Just over a year after the rains began in this same month, Noah's family hears God say, “Go out from the ark” (Genesis 8:16). See it through their eyes: a door opened, animals stepping down one by one, and ground at last firm under their feet — a world washed quiet, and life beginning again.

Through their eyes, a Sabbath of rest.

Today is Shabbat, and in this season the reading is Chayei Sara (Genesis 23–25) — Sarah buried at Hebron, Rebekah brought to Isaac. In a hill-country village no plow moves, no loom sounds, no fire is kindled. Families walk to hear the words read aloud, eat what was prepared yesterday, and let even the ox and the donkey rest. Children learn the story by hearing it again at home, told and retold until it is theirs.

A quiet month, looking ahead.

Cheshvan still has no feast of its own. On Monday, 29 Cheshvan, Ethiopian Jews keep Sigd, longing for Jerusalem, and Hanukkah begins at sundown Friday, December 4 — 27 days away. For now the day closes on a rooftop in the cool air, and the Shema is spoken over a house at peace.

Friend, God still brings His people out onto dry ground — and teaches them to rest there, held in His care.
