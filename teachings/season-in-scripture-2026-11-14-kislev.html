This Season in Scripture — Mercy, Not Just Fasting

The question arrives.

Scripture dates it to today: “In the fourth year of King Darius, the word of the LORD came to Zechariah on the fourth day of the ninth month, which is Chislev” (Zechariah 7:1, ESV). Through their eyes, the men from Bethel — about ten miles north, a hard walk in hill-country mud — stand in Jerusalem asking whether to keep fasting as they have for seventy years. God’s answer turns to their neighbors: “Render true judgments and show kindness and mercy to one another, do not oppress the widow, the fatherless, the sojourner, or the poor” (Zechariah 7:9–10, ESV).

Through their eyes, on Shabbat.

Around this season, today is rest. No sower goes out, no plow cuts the wet hills. Families gather to hear Toldot read — Isaac, and the twins Jacob and Esau (Genesis 25:19–28:9) — children listening close, then a meal made ready yesterday, barley bread and lentils kept warm, lamps lit before sundown. At the table a father repeats the Shema with his children (Deuteronomy 6:7).

What is coming.

Hanukkah begins at sundown Friday, December 4 — 20 days away, on 25 Kislev. Households keep guarding their new olive oil for those dark nights.

Friend, God is not after empty fasting — show His mercy at your own table today, and rest in Him.
