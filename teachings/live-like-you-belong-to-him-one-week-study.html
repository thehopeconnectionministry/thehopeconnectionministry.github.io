Built on Colossians 3:12–17 — the put-on half of the chapter. Verses 5–9 say what comes off; verses 12–17 say what goes on. God does not strip a man and leave him bare — He clothes His own.

The week at a glance

  • Day 1 — The Wardrobe: chosen, holy, beloved — then dressed like it (Colossians 3:12)
  • Day 2 — Bear and Forgive: the Lord's forgiveness sets the pattern (Colossians 3:13)
  • Day 3 — Above All, Love: the garment that binds the rest (Colossians 3:14)
  • Day 4 — Let Peace Rule: peace as the umpire of the heart (Colossians 3:15)
  • Day 5 — The Word Dwelling In: richly, not barely (Colossians 3:16a)
  • Day 6 — Sing, and Do All in His Name (Colossians 3:16b–17)
  • Day 7 — The Full Piece: read "Live Like You Belong to Him" in full, then the end-of-week reflection (below)

One day at a time. Miss a day, pick up the next one — no catch-up pile, no guilt. Set to begin Monday, November 30, 2026 — the week after "Arrest the Thought, Fill the Mind."

Day 1 — The Wardrobe

Read: Colossians 3:12 Focus: Before Paul names a single garment, he names who is wearing them: "God's chosen ones, holy and beloved." The clothing follows the identity — you dress like who you already are in Christ, not to become someone else. The first five pieces: compassionate hearts, kindness, humility, meekness, and patience. One question: Which of the five do you most often leave hanging in the closet — and what would today look like with it on?

Day 2 — Bear and Forgive

Read: Colossians 3:13 Focus: "Bearing with one another" comes before forgiving — some days you simply carry a person you cannot fix. And when there is a real complaint, the pattern is not their apology; it is His pardon: "as the Lord has forgiven you, so you also must forgive." Forgiveness runs downhill from the cross. One question: Is there someone you are still waiting on to make the first move — and what does "as the Lord has forgiven you" say to that wait?

Day 3 — Above All, Love

Read: Colossians 3:14 Focus: Love is not one more item on the list; it is the garment over all the others, "which binds everything together in perfect harmony." Without it, patience turns cold, kindness turns mechanical, and forgiveness turns into bookkeeping. With it, the whole outfit holds together. One question: Which virtue from this week is hardest for you to wear toward the people closest to you — and what would love over it change?

Day 4 — Let Peace Rule

Read: Colossians 3:15 Focus: The word Paul uses for "rule" pictures an umpire — the one who makes the call. The peace of Christ is not a feeling to chase; it is a referee to obey. When anxiety and peace argue in the same heart, peace gets the whistle — because we were "called in one body" to it. And the verse ends with the discipline underneath it all: "And be thankful." One question: Where is a dispute going on in your heart right now — and have you let peace make the call, or is anxiety still holding the whistle?

Day 5 — The Word Dwelling In

Read: Colossians 3:16a Focus: "Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly." Not visit. Not pass through. Dwell — live there, richly, with room to spare. A heart where the Word only visits is a heart furnished by whatever else moved in. This is the same fight as the memory verses: what dwells in you is what comes out of you — teaching, admonishing, even singing. One question: If your heart were a house, what has actually been dwelling in it this week — and what would "richly" add?

Day 6 — Sing, and Do All in His Name

Read: Colossians 3:16b–17 Focus: The passage ends wide open: singing with thankfulness, and then everything — "whatever you do, in word or deed" — done in the name of the Lord Jesus. No corner of the day is left secular. The smallest task, done in His name and with thanks, is worn as worship. One question: What is one ordinary thing on today's list you have never once done "in the name of the Lord Jesus" on purpose — and what changes if you do?

Day 7 — The Full Piece and the Week's Reflection

Read: "Live Like You Belong to Him" (the full piece, below), slowly, with a pen. Then sit with the reflection below. Read each question, answer it in your own words first, then read the answer given.

Live Like You Belong to Him — The Full Piece

Colossians 3 is one chapter with two closets. In the first, Paul names what comes off: "Put to death therefore what is earthly in you" — anger, wrath, malice, slander, the old self with its practices (Colossians 3:5–9). That is the bad news told honestly: the old man has clothes, and they stink of the grave. But the chapter does not leave a man standing stripped. The good news is that God clothes His own — and He starts with who they are before He ever mentions what they wear: "Put on then, as God's chosen ones, holy and beloved, compassionate hearts, kindness, humility, meekness, and patience" (Colossians 3:12).

The verb Paul reaches for is endysasthe — clothe yourselves, put on, the ordinary word for getting dressed in the morning. It is the same word used for putting on Christ Himself (Romans 13:14). That is no accident. The virtues of this passage are not a costume sewn to impress God or men; they are Christ's own character, worn by people who already belong to Him. You do not dress this way to be chosen. You dress this way because you are — chosen, holy, beloved. Identity first, then wardrobe. Get that order backwards and the Christian life becomes a fashion show performed for an audience; get it right and it becomes a son dressing like his Father's house.

Notice how practical the clothing is. It is not woven for a stage; it is woven for a kitchen table and a hard conversation. Bearing with one another (v. 13) — carrying the people who rub you wrong, before there is even anything to forgive. Forgiving one another — not when they have earned it, not when they have apologized well, but "as the Lord has forgiven you." The measure of my forgiveness toward others is not their sorrow; it is His pardon toward me, which came first, at full price, while I was still His enemy. And above all these, love (v. 14) — the outer garment, the one that binds everything together in perfect harmony, so that patience does not curdle into coldness and kindness does not run on empty.

Then the inside of the man gets furnished. "Let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts" (v. 15) — peace promoted from a feeling to an authority, the umpire who makes the call when fear and faith argue. "Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly" (v. 16) — the Word moved in as a resident, not received as a guest. And a heart furnished like that cannot help its overflow: teaching, admonishing, singing psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, thankfulness running through all of it like thread through cloth.

The last verse is the widest door in the passage: "And whatever you do, in word or deed, do everything in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God the Father through him" (v. 17). Whatever you do. There is no leftover category — no hour of the day where the wardrobe comes off. The man who belongs to Christ does not have a church self and a real self. He has one life, lived in one Name, with thanks.

So live like you belong to Him — because you do. Not to earn the belonging, but because the belonging is already settled by the blood of the Son who was stripped so that you could be clothed. The grave clothes are off. The new ones are laid out every morning. Put them on.

End of Week — Reflect: Questions & Answers

Q1: Who does Paul say you are before he tells you what to put on — and why does the order matter? A: "God's chosen ones, holy and beloved" (Colossians 3:12). The order is everything: the clothing follows the identity. We do not dress this way to become God's; we dress this way because we already are His in Christ. Backwards, it is performance. In order, it is a son wearing his Father's likeness.

Q2: What sets the measure for forgiving someone who has wronged you? A: Not their apology, and not your feelings — His pardon: "as the Lord has forgiven you, so you also must forgive" (Colossians 3:13). He forgave first, fully, at the cost of the cross. Forgiveness that waits to be earned is not the kind we received, so it cannot be the kind we give.

Q3: What does love do that the other virtues cannot do alone? A: It binds them. "Above all these put on love, which binds everything together in perfect harmony" (Colossians 3:14). Patience without love turns cold; kindness without love turns mechanical; forgiveness without love turns into bookkeeping. Love is the garment over all the others that holds the whole outfit together.

Q4: What does it mean for the peace of Christ to "rule" in your heart? A: The word pictures an umpire — the one who makes the call. "Let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts, to which indeed you were called in one body. And be thankful" (Colossians 3:15). Peace is not a mood to wait for; it is an authority to yield to. When fear and faith argue, peace holds the whistle.

Q5: How much of the day does verse 17 actually cover? A: All of it. "Whatever you do, in word or deed, do everything in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God the Father through him" (Colossians 3:17). No secular corner, no off-duty hour. The smallest task done in His name, with thanks, is worn as worship by a man who belongs to Him.

Closing prayer for the week: Lord, You have not left me in the grave clothes. You chose me, made me holy, called me beloved — now teach me to dress like it. Put compassion on my heart, forgiveness on my lips, love over it all, and let Your peace make the calls in me. Whatever I do this week, let it be done in the name of the Lord Jesus, with thanks. Amen.
