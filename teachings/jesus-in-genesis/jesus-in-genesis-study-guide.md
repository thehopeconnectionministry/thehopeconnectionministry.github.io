# Jesus in Genesis — Study Guide

**The Hope Connection**  
**Lewis Fuller**

A fifty-chapter reading plan for the book of Genesis, drawn from *Jesus in Genesis — A Chapter-by-Chapter Commentary*. Use it on its own with just your Bible, or alongside the commentary.

*"I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring and her offspring; he shall bruise your head, and you shall bruise his heel."* — Genesis 3:15 (ESV)

---

## How to Use This Guide

**The order of every session.** First, read the chapter in your own Bible — all the way through, out loud if you can — before you read anything else. The guide serves the Scripture; it never replaces it. Then read the commentary section for that chapter if you have it, beginning with "Jesus in this chapter," so your eyes are fixed on Christ before anything else. Then sit with the chapter's question below. Answer it in prayer, out loud to God, and write your answer down.

**Keep a notebook.** A simple notebook is all you need: your answer to each chapter's question, the memory verse written out, and anything the Lord shows you along the way. You might also keep a running page for the promises — every time God repeats His promise to Abraham's family, write it down with its reference. A truth is not really yours until you have written it, prayed it, and lived it.

**The rhythm: three sessions a week.** One chapter per session, three sessions a week, and you will walk through all of Genesis in about seventeen weeks. If you want to go slower, spread one chapter across the whole week: read it one day, study it the next, and pray through the question on the third. Either pace is fine — what matters is the steadiness, not the speed.

**No catch-up piles.** If you miss a day, you do not owe anybody two chapters. Just pick up at the next chapter at the next session. Life happens — sickness, family, work, hard weeks. This rhythm bends and keeps going. The Lord is not keeping a tally, and neither are we.

**Alone, together, or in a group.** Alone: one Bible, one notebook, and prayer before and after. With a spouse or a friend: read the chapter aloud together, talk the question through honestly, and pray for each other out of what it showed you. In a small group: one chapter per meeting, read it aloud to open, save real time at the end for the question, and let the memory verse be the group's verse for the week.

**Read in the Reformation way.** Hold to the five watchwords of the Reformers as you go: *sola scriptura* — Scripture alone is the rule; *sola gratia* — grace alone saves; *sola fide* — faith alone receives it; *solus Christus* — Christ alone is the Savior; *soli Deo gloria* — glory to God alone. And in every chapter, keep the law and the gospel straight: the law shows us our sin and our need; the gospel shows us Christ and His finished work. Genesis will do the rest.

### Study Questions — One per Chapter

Work each question in your notebook after the chapter's reflection question. Many of them follow the Bible's own pattern of putting off the old and putting on the new (Ephesians 4:22–24; Colossians 3:8–10).

1. **Genesis 1 — Made in His image.** Before anything was wrong, everything was good, and you were made in God's image. Put off one way you have believed a lie about your worth; put on this practice: thank God out loud today for one specific way He made you on purpose.
2. **Genesis 2 — Not good to be alone.** God built marriage and rest into creation before sin entered. Put off one habit of isolating yourself; put on one step toward honest fellowship — a meal, a call, a confession — this week. Name it.
3. **Genesis 3 — The Seed is promised.** Adam and Eve hid; God came seeking. Put off one place where you are hiding from God; put on coming into the open in prayer today, telling Him exactly where you are, as He asked Adam: "Where are you?"
4. **Genesis 4 — Rule over it.** Sin crouched at Cain's door, and Cain opened it. What sin is crouching at your door right now? Put off feeding it; put on one concrete guard — a door closed, a number blocked, a brother told — this week.
5. **Genesis 5 — Walk with God.** In a chapter of "and he died," Enoch walked with God. Put off one time-wasting pattern; put on a daily walk with God — a set time, a set place — and write down when it will happen.
6. **Genesis 6 — Find favor, obey fully.** Noah found grace and then obeyed in detail for years before rain fell. Put off partial obedience; put on finishing one command of God you have left half-done. Which one?
7. **Genesis 7 — Shut in by God.** Noah obeyed; God shut the door. Put off anxiety over the doors in your life; put on trust: name one situation you will leave in God's hands this week instead of forcing.
8. **Genesis 8 — God remembers.** "God remembered Noah" did not mean He had forgotten. Put off the belief that your waiting means God's absence; put on building an altar — one deliberate act of thanksgiving for one mercy in the waiting.
9. **Genesis 9 — Look at the bow.** God hung His war bow in the clouds, pointing away from the earth. Put off fear that God's anger is aimed at you in Christ; put on looking up: the next time judgment anxiety rises, say Genesis 9:13 out loud.
10. **Genesis 10 — Every nation matters.** God kept the names of seventy nations, not just one family. Put off a narrow heart; put on praying this week for one nation or people group by name — and for one neighbor not like you.
11. **Genesis 11 — Babel or blessing.** Men built to make a name for themselves; God gave Abram a name by grace. Put off one project of self-naming; put on receiving: write down one gift from God you did nothing to earn, and thank Him for it.
12. **Genesis 12 — Go, and be a blessing.** Abram left everything on a promise. Put off one security you are clutching; put on one act of blessing toward "all families" — someone outside your usual circle — this week.
13. **Genesis 13 — Choose like Abram.** Lot chose by sight and drifted toward Sodom; Abram chose peace and trusted the promise. Put off choosing by appetite; put on choosing by promise: name one decision before you now and how the promise, not the view, will decide it.
14. **Genesis 14 — Refuse the spoil.** Abram took bread and wine from Melchizedek and refused riches from Sodom's king. Put off one gain that would own you; put on tithe-hearted generosity: give first to God this week before spending on yourself.
15. **Genesis 15 — Believe the LORD.** Righteousness was counted to Abram because he believed, not because he performed. Put off one place where you are trying to earn God's acceptance; put on resting: say Genesis 15:6 over yourself in prayer today.
16. **Genesis 16 — The God who sees.** Hagar, a runaway slave, named God "You are a God of seeing." Put off believing you are invisible in your trouble; put on calling on El Roi by name in prayer — and looking for one overlooked person to see this week.
17. **Genesis 17 — Walk before Me.** God renamed Abram at ninety-nine, when everything looked impossible. Put off the deadline you have set on God's promises; put on walking before Him today in one ordinary duty done as unto Him.
18. **Genesis 18 — Plead for the city.** Abraham stood before the LORD and pleaded for Sodom. Put off writing off a person or place as too far gone; put on intercession: pray by name this week for someone others have given up on.
19. **Genesis 19 — Don't look back.** Lot's wife looked back and froze. Put off one longing glance at the life God is bringing you out of; put on running: name the mercy carrying you (Genesis 19:29 says God remembered) and thank Him for it.
20. **Genesis 20 — Grace after failure.** Abraham repeated an old sin, and God still guarded the promise. Put off despair over a repeated failure; put on fresh repentance — and one safeguard so the same fear does not drive the same sin again.
21. **Genesis 21 — Laughter arrives.** Sarah laughed in unbelief in chapter 18 and in joy in chapter 21. Put off cynicism about God's timing; put on keeping a written list of prayers you are still waiting on — and one line of thanks for the ones already answered.
22. **Genesis 22 — The LORD will provide.** Abraham surrendered the dearest thing and found a substitute provided. Put off withholding your "Isaac" — the person, plan, or possession you trust more than God; put on open hands in prayer today, naming it and releasing it to Him.
23. **Genesis 23 — Faith buys a grave.** Abraham's first owned land in Canaan was a tomb: he believed God while burying his wife. Put off demanding to see the whole promise now; put on one act of faith in a place that looks like loss — a prayer, a gift, a word of hope spoken over it.
24. **Genesis 24 — Guided step by step.** The servant prayed a specific prayer and watched God lead. Put off anxious control of a decision; put on the servant's way: pray specifically, then take the next open step and note how God answers.
25. **Genesis 25 — Don't despise the birthright.** Esau sold forever for a stew. Put off one appetite that trades the lasting for the immediate; put on remembering before you are hungry — decide now, in writing, what you will do when that appetite speaks.
26. **Genesis 26 — Dig again.** Isaac reopened his father's wells instead of fighting over them. Put off contention over a stopped-up well in your life; put on digging again: return to one neglected practice of grace — prayer, the Word, fellowship — this week.
27. **Genesis 27 — The blessing and the deceit.** God carried His purpose through a deceitful scheme without excusing the deceit. Put off scheming to get God's gifts your way; put on honesty: confess one manipulation, large or small, to God — and to the person if needed.
28. **Genesis 28 — God in this place.** Jacob woke in an ordinary field and found it the house of God. Put off the belief that God is only in special places; put on awareness: tonight, name the ordinary place where God met you today.
29. **Genesis 29 — Judah from Leah.** The royal line came through the unloved wife who learned to praise. Put off measuring your worth by being chosen or noticed; put on Leah's word — "This time I will praise the LORD" — and praise Him for one unnoticed mercy.
30. **Genesis 30 — God remembers.** Rachel's years of waiting ended with "God remembered Rachel." Put off the scorekeeping of a long wait; put on asking God plainly for the desire of your heart, and leaving the timing written in His hands, not yours.
31. **Genesis 31 — God has seen my affliction.** Jacob testified that God saw and rebuked on his behalf. Put off rehearsing a wrong done to you in order to repay it; put on committing your case to God in prayer — and leaving Laban's territory, in heart if not in geography.
32. **Genesis 32 — Wrestle and limp.** Jacob came away blessed and limping. Put off demanding that God bless you without changing you; put on clinging: in prayer this week, refuse to let go of one promise of God until you have His blessing on it.
33. **Genesis 33 — The face of God in a brother.** Jacob saw God's face in Esau's embrace. Put off delaying a reconciliation; put on going first: name one person, and take the first step toward peace this week, however small.
34. **Genesis 34 — Grief without revenge.** Dinah's brothers answered evil with worse evil. Put off answering wrong with wrong; put on grieving honestly before God — bring one injustice to Him in prayer and leave the vengeance in His hands (Deuteronomy 32:35).
35. **Genesis 35 — Put away the foreign gods.** Jacob's household buried its idols under an oak before going up to Bethel. Put off keeping one household idol — object, habit, or subscription; put on burying it: literally remove it this week, and go up to worship.
36. **Genesis 36 — God's kindness to Esau's line.** Edom had kings while Israel was still a family. Put off envy when others advance faster; put on thanking God for one blessing in the life of someone whose path is not yours.
37. **Genesis 37 — The beloved son betrayed.** Joseph's brothers hated the favored son. Put off envy of a favored brother or sister; put on this: if you are the one in the pit, write down Joseph's story in one sentence — betrayed, not abandoned — and pray it back to God.
38. **Genesis 38 — Judah confesses.** Judah's turning began with three honest words' worth of confession: "She is more righteous than I." Put off covering a shameful chapter; put on Judah's confession — admit your part plainly to God, and where needed, to the person wronged.
39. **Genesis 39 — The LORD was with Joseph.** In a prison Joseph kept his integrity and his usefulness. Put off the belief that faithfulness only counts in good circumstances; put on doing excellent, honest work in the place you are — as unto the LORD who is with you.
40. **Genesis 40 — Forgotten, not forsaken.** The cupbearer forgot Joseph for two full years. Put off bitterness at being forgotten by people; put on remembering that God's memory is not man's — write down one way God has remembered you while men forgot.
41. **Genesis 41 — It is not in me.** Joseph refused the credit and gave God the answer. Put off claiming glory for God's gifts in you; put on Joseph's sentence — "It is not in me" — and use it, in some form, the next time you are praised.
42. **Genesis 42 — Guilt remembered.** Twenty years on, the brothers' guilt woke up. Put off burying an old sin deeper; put on bringing it up — confess one long-ago wrong to God today, and if the person still lives and it is safe, begin to make it right.
43. **Genesis 43 — Treasure in the sacks.** The brothers feared a kindness they did not understand. Put off suspecting every mercy; put on receiving grace as grace: name one unearned kindness in your life and thank the Giver without trying to repay it.
44. **Genesis 44 — Judah stands in the gap.** The man who sold Joseph now offers himself as a slave for Benjamin. Put off protecting yourself at a brother's expense; put on standing in the gap: name one person you can shield, vouch for, or carry this week — and do it.
45. **Genesis 45 — God sent me.** Joseph wept, forgave, and fed the brothers who sold him. Put off rehearsing the betrayal; put on Joseph's theology: write down the worst thing done to you, and beside it, one way God has used it to preserve life.
46. **Genesis 46 — Do not be afraid to go down.** God met Jacob at Beersheba before the descent into Egypt. Put off fear of the next season; put on worship before you move: stop at your own Beersheba — pray and offer thanks before the change, not only after it.
47. **Genesis 47 — A sojourner's honesty.** Jacob called his years "few and evil" and still blessed Pharaoh. Put off both complaining and pretending; put on Jacob's honesty: tell God the truth about your years today, and then bless someone from inside the hardship.
48. **Genesis 48 — The Shepherd all my life long.** Dying Jacob's summary of God was one word: Shepherd. Put off summarizing your life by its worst chapters; put on Jacob's summary: write your own one-sentence testimony of God's shepherding, and keep it in your Bible.
49. **Genesis 49 — The scepter and the blessing.** Jacob's blessings were prophecy: Judah's line would carry the King. Put off a small view of your words to the next generation; put on blessing: speak one deliberate, Scripture-shaped blessing over a child, grandchild, or young believer this week.
50. **Genesis 50 — God meant it for good.** Joseph died trusting a promise he would not live to see kept. Put off demanding the final chapter now; put on Joseph's faith: name one unfinished promise you will entrust to God beyond your own lifetime — and rest.
