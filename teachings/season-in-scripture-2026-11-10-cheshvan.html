This Season in Scripture — A New Moon Over Wet Furrows

Rosh Chodesh Kislev — day one.

Because Cheshvan runs 30 days this year, the new month is kept two days: today, and tomorrow, 1 Kislev. Eyes lift at dusk for a thin crescent. In Temple days, trumpets sound over the new-moon offerings (Numbers 10:10; 28:11–15), and David’s empty seat at Saul’s new-moon feast is noticed at once (1 Samuel 20). “Blow the trumpet at the new moon, at the full moon, on our feast day” (Psalm 81:3, ESV).

Bad news carried into the new month.

Scripture dates Nehemiah’s news to this month, not a day: in the month of Chislev, men from Judah reach him in Susa (Nehemiah 1:1–2) with word that Jerusalem’s wall is broken down. Nehemiah weeps, fasts, and prays — bad news that points him to God.

Through their eyes, in the fields.

On the Gezer farmer’s calendar, Kislev opens the two months of planting. Around this season a Judean farmer walks wet furrows behind his oxen, sowing barley and wheat into rain-softened soil, his son scattering seed and learning the trade beside him. Wadis can run with runoff, hill roads turn slick, lamps are lit earlier, and the Shema is spoken morning and evening.

What is coming.

Hanukkah begins at sundown Friday, December 4 — 24 days away, on 25 Kislev. No pilgrim road fills; it is a home festival, so families check their lamps and guard their new oil.

Friend, a new month begins the old faithful way — look up, pray, sow in wet ground, and trust God with what grows.
