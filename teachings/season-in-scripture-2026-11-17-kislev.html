This Season in Scripture — 7 Kislev 5787: News From Home in the Month of Kislev

News from home.

Around this season, Nehemiah is far from Jerusalem, serving in Susa. Scripture says, “Now it happened in the month of Chislev, in the twentieth year, as I was in Susa the citadel, that Hanani, one of my brothers, came with certain men from Judah” (Nehemiah 1:1–2, ESV). Through their eyes: travelers arrive muddy from the long road, and the news they carry is heavy — “The wall of Jerusalem is broken down, and its gates are destroyed by fire” (Nehemiah 1:3, ESV). Nehemiah weeps, fasts, and prays. Bad news, in the darkest month, becomes the start of rebuilding.

Through their eyes, earning a living.

Between rains, a sower hurries back to the field to finish the late sowing of wheat and barley while the ground is soft — too wet and the seed rots, too long and the season slips. Indoors, a mother stretches oil, lentils, and barley flour, and a boy learns his father’s trade at the bench while rain drums the flat roof.

The land itself.

Days are short now. Lamps are lit early, a charcoal brazier warms the winter room (Jeremiah 36:22), cloaks stay on indoors, and Hermon can whiten while the hill roads run slick. At dawn and dusk the household speaks the Shema (Deuteronomy 6:7), and on long evenings stories and songs gather the family around the fire.

What is coming.

Hanukkah begins at sundown Friday, December 4 — 17 days away, on 25 Kislev. Households still clean their lamps and guard their best new olive oil for those nights.

Friend, when hard news reaches you far from where you wish you were, do what Nehemiah did — bring it to God first, and let prayer become the first stone of rebuilding.
