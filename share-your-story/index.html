Have You Ever Felt Truly Alone?

There Is Hope — my testimony. Thirty years of addiction, and the night Jesus got hold of me.

Have you ever felt truly alone? Not just by yourself — alone in a way that sits down inside you and stays there. You can be in a room full of people and still feel unseen, unknown, and unhelped. I lived in that feeling for a very long time.

My name is Lewis. I am not a pastor, not a scholar. I am a man whose life was wrecked by over 30 years of hard drug addiction — until Jesus Christ got hold of me, and it changed everything.

I am not talking to you from above your struggle. I have been in the pit, and I know the way out has a name.

The night everything changed

John 17 is often called the High Priestly prayer of Jesus. It is the night before the cross. Jesus has shared the Last Supper with his disciples, and on the way to the garden where he will be arrested, he prays out loud with them listening. He prays for himself, for his disciples standing right there — and then, in verse 20, he looks further than they could see: "I do not ask for these only, but also for those who will believe in me through their word." That is you. That is me. Two thousand years before we were born, Jesus was praying for everyone who would ever believe.

And look at how Jesus defines eternal life: "that they know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent." Not knowing about God — personal, living knowledge, the way you know a close friend. Eternal life is not first a place. It is a person.

I was in a men's discipleship program, reading John 17 verses 3 and 20, when it was as if a light lit up in my head. I understood what it said. I believed it. I trusted that God's Word was true — and I knew, I knew, that God had changed my life right there. I could not keep it in. When God opens blind eyes, you do not sit quietly. You tell someone.

I remember that first night, sitting on my bed, crying like a baby. I felt the arms of God wrapping around me. It was refreshing, and I didn't want it to end. He has me. He is still going to have me. He has never left, and He is never going to let go — no matter how far I stray, He will always welcome me back. And He can do the same for you.

No gloss: the struggle didn't end there

Let me be honest with you, because I will not gloss over this. I have had my struggles since the day I came to Christ. It didn't make me perfect, and it didn't make my life easy. There have been hard days since then. But here is the difference, and it changes everything: I'm not alone in this anymore. I know Jesus now. And on the days I cannot hold on, He holds me. My grip fails. His doesn't.

Four truths that carried me

1. God rewards those who seek him (Hebrews 11:6). Not those who have it all together. Not those with a clean record. Those who seek. Seeking doesn't mean perfect seeking — it means you keep showing up. I kept showing up, and He met me. And notice what the reward is: not mainly sobriety or a better life. The Rewarder gives himself. I was looking for the gifts when I needed the Giver. The day I sought the Giver, I found everything.

2. Jesus prayed for you before you existed (John 17:20–21). He was praying for me — the dropout, the addict, the man who had walked in and out of programs. Two thousand years before I was born, Jesus prayed that I would believe. Friend, He prayed for you too. You are not an afterthought. You cannot be forgotten by someone who prayed for you before you were born.

3. A new creation by grace, not effort (2 Corinthians 5:17). The old Lewis didn't get renovated — he died, and the new man was raised. "If anyone is in Christ" — that word has no exceptions. Not for people with records, addictions, or forty failures. Your past doesn't get the final word over your life. Christ has the final word.

4. The gospel in one sentence (Romans 5:8). God did not wait for me to get clean, finish school, or fix my record. While I was still a sinner — thirty years deep — Christ died for me. Grace isn't a reward for the deserving. It's a gift for the desperate. I was desperate. Desperate people are exactly who Jesus came for.

Your worst chapter is not your final chapter when Christ holds the pen.

What this means for you today

You do not have to get your life together before you come to him. That is backwards. You come to him because you cannot get it together. He doesn't ask for your résumé. He asks for your trust. Come as you are — empty, addicted, ashamed, afraid — and let the One who died for sinners do what only He can.

One simple response: ask yourself, what am I still trying to fix in my own strength? Name it plainly. Confess it to God honestly — He already knows, and He is not shocked. Trust him with the next step, not the whole staircase. Just the next step.

Seeking him is not complicated. Open this Book. Get on your knees. Say, "Lord, I want to know you." He rewards that. Don't wait until you feel ready — you will never feel ready. You can't always go by your feelings, but you can go by God's truth. He will not let you down.

You are never too far gone. If He could reach a man who spent 30 years riding addiction — homeless, hopeless, alone, locked up in his own prison — He can reach you right where you are.
